What Wood means for the Tiger

Wood is the element of growth, expansion, and forward motion. The Tiger is the most Wood-coded large animal in the cycle, which is why the fit matters. Place a Tiger in a hot, dry, urban city and the restless energy turns into a tax.

The practical reads: places with serious outdoor access, cool to moderate climate, four real seasons, and a community that values showing up in person. The Pacific Northwest, the upper Midwest, the mountain west, and the rolling Northeast all hit the right notes.

The element is a fit filter, not a rule. A Tiger can live outside the Wood list. The cost is that the climate and the element pull in different directions, and a Tiger ends up working harder to align with the place.

A Tiger in a cool, green city with mountain access is a Tiger in flow. A Tiger in a hot, dry suburb is a Tiger running on battery.

The Reasoning

Why These Regions Fit a Tiger

Four reasons, all rooted in the Wood element and the way a Tiger actually runs day to day.

1

Outdoor space and trail access

The Tiger recharges by moving. Not by sitting in coffee shops. A city with real parks, a working trail system, and weekend terrain within an hour keeps the sign in flow. Boulder, Bend, Bozeman, Asheville —the trail starts at the trailhead, not the parking lot.

2

Cold weather and clean air

Tigers run better in 30 to 70 degrees. Not in 90-plus humidity. A cool, dry, four-season climate matches the way the sign actually operates. Phoenix in July is a Tiger in a slow oven. Bozeman in February is a Tiger in flow.

3

Four real seasons

Tigers like the rhythm of fall, winter, spring, and summer. A climate that turns through each one keeps the schedule visible and the wardrobe working. San Diego works, but the Tiger misses the season cues. New England, the Mountain West, the Great Lakes —all keep the calendar honest.

4

A pace that rewards competition

A working sports, outdoor, or competitive scene —without the city running on status. The Tiger feeds on the win, not the visible car. Boulder, Portland, Minneapolis. The sign runs faster where the playing field is the score.

Burlington, VT
