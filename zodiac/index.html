What Is the Chinese Zodiac?

The chinese zodiac is a twelve-year cycle that maps one animal to each year. It runs back to the Han Dynasty and is still the most common reference for personality patterns, family reading, and yearly cycle tracking across East Asia.

The chinese zodiac is built on a simple rule. Every year links to one of twelve animals, and the order is fixed: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. Once the cycle reaches the Pig, it starts over at the Rat.

One detail trips people up: the year does not start on January 1. It begins on Chinese New Year, which lands between January 21 and February 20 on the Gregorian calendar. A January or early-February birthday often belongs to the previous year's sign, and the cutoff shifts slightly year to year. The exact date window for any birth year is listed on its own page further down.

On top of the animals, five elements rotate through the cycle: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. The animal and the element together create a 60-year pattern, so no two year combinations repeat within a single lifetime. Two people born in the same animal year can still read quite differently once the element layer is factored in. For a closer look at how each sign plays out across the trait side, the personality section breaks each of the 12 animals down separately.

The 12 Chinese Zodiac Animals

Each animal in the cycle carries a working trait pattern. The list below is a quick read of what tends to repeat across people born in the same year.

Rat

Quick, observant, and socially sharp. The Rat is the opener of the twelve-year cycle and tends to read a room before anyone else has finished walking into it.

Ox

Steady, patient, and built for long hauls. The Ox sign is the slow-burn profile: late to start, hard to stop, and a reliable worker under pressure.

Tiger

Bold, direct, and comfortable at the front. The Tiger sign runs on action and a low tolerance for wasted time, and the leadership read comes naturally.

Rabbit

Gentle, careful, and even-tempered. The Rabbit sign is the diplomat read: a soft voice, a sharp eye, and a long memory for how people treat each other.

Dragon

Charismatic, ambitious, and hard to miss. The Dragon sign is the high-visibility profile: big ideas, big energy, and a stage that tends to find them.

Snake

Quiet, perceptive, and a strong reader of the room. The Snake sign is the strategist profile: calm on the outside, working a few moves ahead underneath.

Horse

Active, restless, and independent. The Horse sign needs room to move and works best when the leash stays loose, both at work and at home.

Goat

Soft-spoken, creative, and thoughtful. The Goat sign is the caretaker read: a strong eye for design, comfort, and the people who need looking after.

Monkey

Curious, fast on the uptake, and playful. The Monkey sign is the quick-study read: bored by routine, sharp on the first pass, and three steps ahead in conversation.

Rooster

Practical, observant, and well-organized. The Rooster sign is the do-it-right profile: clean execution, a strong work ethic, and an eye for detail others miss.

Dog

Steady, fair, and slow to trust. The Dog sign is the loyalty read: honest in plain terms, a hard worker, and a long-haul friend once the relationship is set.

Pig

Warm, generous, and easy company. The Pig sign is the easygoing profile: a soft spot for good food, a wide social circle, and a habit of giving more than the room deserves.

How does the year-by-year list work?

When does the year start?
