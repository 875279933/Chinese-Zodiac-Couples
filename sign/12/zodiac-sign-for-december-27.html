They were impulsive in the small ways and deliberate in the big ones, and they kept a notebook with the temperature and humidity of every day they had worked in the studio, going back to 2011, because some things were just better that way.

On their birthday, Lorelei Foster spent the afternoon in the studio, then on the porch, then back in the studio, and called the whole thing a good day. They had a running list of things they wanted to get to before the next decade: replace the kiln, take the trip to the coast, fix the screen door. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Lorelei Foster was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Lorelei Foster found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram.
