A Taurus Story — Mason, May 3

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Mason Kirkland was 46 and had lived in the same St. Louis apartment for 11 years. They worked as a bartender, and they liked it. Not in a dramatic way. In a 'I know where the good parking is and my coworker brings donuts on Fridays' kind of way. Mason was terrible at texting back, and their apartment had a record player that gets used more than any appliance, which told you almost everything you needed to know.

On their birthday, Mason got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Mason ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

Before falling asleep, Mason wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On May 3, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Mason thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
