A Gemini Story — Nora, May 21

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Nora Merriman was 44, worked as a nurse in Seattle, and had, in the last 12 months alone, taken up pottery, birdwatching, competitive trivia, and a short-lived attempt to learn Portuguese. None of them stuck. Nora was famous for a story they don't like to tell, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Nora spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on May 21, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Nora found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Gemini, Nora thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
