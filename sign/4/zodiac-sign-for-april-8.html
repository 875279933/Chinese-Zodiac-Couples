A Aries Story — Margot, April 8

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Margot Zimmerman turned 38 on a Tuesday and, being an Aries, had already made and abandoned three birthday plans before 9 a.m. They worked as a bike mechanic in Philadelphia, where they were known for finishing things early and arguing with the copy machine on principle. Margot was unreasonably loyal -- the kind of person who would drive 45 minutes out of the way to try a sandwich someone mentioned, then leave an honest one-star review when it was mid.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Margot had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Margot yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Margot found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Aries, Margot thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
