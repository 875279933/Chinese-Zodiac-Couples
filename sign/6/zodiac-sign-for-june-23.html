A Cancer Story — Hannah, June 23

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Hannah Zimmerman was 34, a barista from Savannah, and the kind of person who still had a record player that gets used more than any appliance from their childhood home. They were surprisingly patient -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Hannah kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Hannah had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Hannah yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Hannah was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 34. The Cancer in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
