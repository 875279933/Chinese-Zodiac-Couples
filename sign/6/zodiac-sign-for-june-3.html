A Gemini Story — Caleb Calloway, June 3

A short, personal read on this day

Caleb Calloway was 28, worked as a small-business owner in San Diego, and had, in the last year, taken up pottery, birdwatching, and a half-finished attempt at Portuguese. None of them stuck. Caleb was famous among their friends, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

On their birthday, Caleb got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Caleb ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Caleb was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 28. The Gemini in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
