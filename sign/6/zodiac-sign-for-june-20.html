A Gemini Story — Nora Albright, June 20

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Story
Nora Albright, on turning a year older

Nora Albright was 34, worked as a wedding photographer in Nashville, and had, in the last year, taken up pottery, birdwatching, and a half-finished attempt at Portuguese. None of them stuck. Nora was a little too honest, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

On their birthday, Nora got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Nora ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

By the time Nora got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, sits in a different sign.
