A Cancer Story — Tyler OConnell, June 25

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Story · Cincinnati

Tyler OConnell was 41, a high school coach from Cincinnati, and the kind of person who still had a kettle that is almost always on from their childhood home. They were quietly competitive -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Tyler kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

On their birthday, Tyler got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Tyler ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

By the time Tyler got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, sits in a different sign.
