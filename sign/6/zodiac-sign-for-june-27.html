A Cancer Story — Jordan, June 27

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Jordan Fernandez was 26, a freelance writer from Richmond, and the kind of person who still had a kettle that is almost always on from their childhood home. They were weirdly good with directions -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Jordan kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

Later that afternoon, Jordan sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 36: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Jordan was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

By the time Jordan got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, is a different sign entirely.
