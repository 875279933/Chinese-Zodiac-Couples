A Cancer Story — Rosalind Krause, June 30

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Story · Savannah

Rosalind Krause was 39, worked as a wedding photographer in Savannah, and had, in the last year, taken up pottery, birdwatching, and a half-finished attempt at Portuguese. None of them stuck. Rosalind was the friend who brings the snacks, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

On their birthday, Rosalind got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Rosalind ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

When the phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Rosalind found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and did not delete any. Being a Cancer, Rosalind thought, was less about the loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
