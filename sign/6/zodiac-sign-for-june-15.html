A Gemini Story — Iris Delgado, June 15

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Story
Iris Delgado, on turning a year older

Iris Delgado was 27, worked as a graphic designer in Brooklyn, and had, in the last year, taken up pottery, birdwatching, and a half-finished attempt at Portuguese. None of them stuck. Iris was quietly stubborn, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Iris spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on June 15, were not among them. It felt, in its own way, like progress.

When the phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Iris found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and did not delete any. Being a Gemini, Iris thought, was less about the loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
