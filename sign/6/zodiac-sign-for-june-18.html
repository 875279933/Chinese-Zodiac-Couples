A Gemini Story — Stella Brennan, June 18

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Story
Stella Brennan, on turning a year older

Stella Brennan was 29, worked as an urban planner in Portland, and had, in the last year, taken up pottery, birdwatching, and a half-finished attempt at Portuguese. None of them stuck. Stella was famous for a story they don't like to tell, and their texts came in paragraphs at 9 a.m. and silence for two days after. They didn't mean anything by it. They were just off somewhere else.

On their birthday, Stella got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Stella ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

When the phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Stella found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and did not delete any. Being a Gemini, Stella thought, was less about the loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
