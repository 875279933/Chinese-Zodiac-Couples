Field Notes — The Library, February 22

Owen Pham was 29, a high school history teacher in Tacoma, and the kind of person who went to the library on a Sunday, which is the kind of thing his students could not quite believe. He had a small apartment, a serious book collection, and a girlfriend who was a graphic designer, who had, on more than one occasion, told him he owned too many books. He had, in his professional view, exactly the right number of books.

On a Sunday in late February, he went to the library, which is to say he went to a library he had not been to in a long time, in a neighborhood he had not been to in years. The library was smaller than he remembered. The librarian was older. He picked up a book he had been meaning to read for two years, sat at a table near the window, and read for an hour.

By the afternoon, he had walked around the neighborhood, bought a coffee at a place he did not recognize, and was standing outside a house that he had lived in as a child. He did not go in. He looked at it for a few minutes. He thought about the family that had lived there for 20 years and what their kitchen might look like. He thought, as he often did on Sundays, that the city was, in fact, a small place. He went home with the book. He read for another hour. The day, in other words, was the right size.
