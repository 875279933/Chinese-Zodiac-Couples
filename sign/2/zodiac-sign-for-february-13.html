A Reader Story — A Birthday Phone Call, February 13

Naomi Park was 27, a graduate student in clinical psychology in Boston, and the older of two sisters by exactly 16 months. Her younger sister, Hana, lived in San Francisco and had a habit of calling at odd times. Naomi had a habit of answering. This was the arrangement, and it had been the arrangement for 27 years, and it was, in her view, a good arrangement. They had good birthdays. Hana's was in March. Naomi's was the 13th of February.

On a Wednesday in mid-February, she woke up, made coffee, and checked her phone. There were 14 messages. There was a text from her mother that said, in her mother's way, that she was being thought of. There was a card from her grandmother that had been mailed three days ago. There was a text from Hana that said, simply, happy birthday, no further comment. Naomi smiled at all of these, in the way you smile at a thing you have known about for 27 years and are still, somehow, surprised by.

She went to campus. She had a meeting with her advisor, a session with a client, a phone call with her father, who asked if she was eating enough, which she took seriously. By 6 o'clock she was home, and Hana had called for real. They talked for 90 minutes, which was their usual. They did not have anything to report. They were, in the way sisters are, simply keeping each other company from across the country. By 9, Naomi was making tea. The day had been the right size. The phone call had been the right size. The year was going to be fine.
