Field Notes — The First Pitch, February 17

Felix Ortiz was 42, a high school baseball coach in the Bronx, and the kind of father who had taught his son to throw a curveball at age 9. His son, Mateo, was now 14 and on the JV team. Felix had been coaching for 16 years. He had won three league championships. He had also lost more games than he had won, and he was, in his own opinion, comfortable with that.

On a Thursday in mid-February, the team had its first outdoor practice of the season. The field was muddy. The air was the kind of cold that gets into your fingers. The kids were, in his professional view, mostly terrible, which was, in his professional view, exactly how they were supposed to be in February. He ran them through a series of drills. He threw batting practice for an hour. He did not yell, which is to say he was in a good mood.

By the afternoon, his hands were cold and his throat was dry and Mateo had hit a double off the fence that he was, very clearly, going to talk about for at least three days. Felix sat in the bleachers with the other coaches, drank bad coffee, and watched the JV run sprints. He thought, as he often did at the first practice of the season, that the year was going to be fine. The kids were terrible. The field was muddy. The coffee was bad. The year was going to be fine.
