A Reader Story — The Pie, February 15

Beatrice Larsen was 67, a retired librarian in Madison, Wisconsin, and the kind of grandmother who had, in her time, made 41 birthday cakes. She had stopped counting at 41. She had a small house with a large kitchen and a husband of 42 years who did most of the gardening and was, in her view, a serious man about tomatoes. She had three grandchildren, all of whom she saw roughly once a month, all of whom had opinions about pie.

On a Saturday in mid-February, she made an apple pie. This was not for a birthday or for a holiday. It was for a Tuesday, which was next week, and which her granddaughter Mira was going to be in town for. The pie was, in her view, the right kind of project for a Saturday in February. The apples were from a friend who had a small orchard. The crust was her mother's recipe, which had been her grandmother's recipe, which had been a recipe that had been, by her best guess, around for a while.

She rolled out the crust at 10, put it in the oven at 11, and pulled it out at 12:30. The pie was, in her professional view, a 9. She set it on the counter to cool and called her daughter to confirm the arrival time. Her husband came in from the garden with dirt on his hands and said, with no particular surprise, that the pie smelled right. They ate a small slice each at 3, with a slice of cheddar, which was how you were supposed to eat apple pie in Wisconsin. By 5 she was reading, he was napping, the dog was asleep in the sun, and the pie was, simply, the right kind of project for a Saturday.
