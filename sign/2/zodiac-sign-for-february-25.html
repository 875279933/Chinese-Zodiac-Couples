A short, personal read on this day

Reader Portrait — The Recipe Card, February 25

Eloise Tremblay was 72, a retired teacher in Burlington, Vermont, and the kind of grandmother who had been writing things down for 50 years. She had a small spiral notebook in her kitchen drawer, a recipe box that had been her mother's, and a stack of letters she had been meaning to organize for 12 years. She had three grandchildren, all in college, all of whom she saw roughly once a month, all of whom had started to ask her for recipes, which is, in her view, a good sign.

On a Tuesday in late February, she sat at the kitchen table with a cup of coffee and her mother's recipe box and copied out six recipes. She had been meaning to do this for a year. The recipes were for things her mother had made on Sundays: a beef stew, a brown bread, a custard that had been, in her professional view, the right kind of project for a Sunday afternoon. She copied them in pencil, on cards she had bought for the purpose, in handwriting that was, in her view, getting worse.

By 4 o'clock, the cards were done. The recipe box was, in fact, lighter than it had been in 40 years. She called her granddaughter in Boston, who answered on the third ring, and told her about the cards. The granddaughter said she would come up next month. Eloise said she would make the stew. They talked for an hour. The cards, in her view, were good. The handwriting was getting worse, but the cards were good. The year, in her view, was going to be fine.
