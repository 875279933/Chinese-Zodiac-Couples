A Pisces Story — Jordan, February 20

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Jordan Nelson was 29, a physical therapist in Tucson, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were the friend who brings the snacks -- the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Jordan kept a stack of library books on the nightstand because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Jordan had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Jordan yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Jordan found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Pisces, Jordan thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
