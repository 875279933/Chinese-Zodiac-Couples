A Reader Story — A Good Sandwich, February 11

Lila Friedman was 51, a community college math teacher in Baltimore, and the kind of woman who remembered birthdays. She had 34 nieces and nephews by marriage, a sister in Denver she talked to every Sunday, and a husband who made a serious pot of coffee every morning and a serious pot of soup on Sundays. Her kitchen had a small chalkboard on which she wrote the menu of the week, more out of habit than necessity.

On a Friday in early February, she got up, went to work, taught two sections of statistics, and came home. Her husband was making the soup. He had been making the soup for years and had, in that time, gotten serious about it. She sat at the counter and graded papers for 45 minutes while he chopped. The kitchen smelled like leeks and bay leaves, and the cat, who had been in the bedroom all day, came downstairs to investigate.

By 7, the soup was on. They ate at the kitchen table, with bread, with butter, with a small salad neither of them had made a project of. Her sister called from Denver and they talked for an hour. Her husband read the paper. The cat slept on the chair nobody was using. By 10, the soup was in the fridge for the weekend and Lila was reading a novel she had been neglecting. The day, in other words, was the right size. The soup was good. The sister was good. The year was going to be fine. She did not need it to be more than fine.
