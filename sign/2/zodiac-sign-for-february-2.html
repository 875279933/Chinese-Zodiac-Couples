A Reader Story — Two Texts and a Coffee, February 2

Diego Santos was 41, a structural engineer in Austin, and the kind of person who repaired his own dishwasher even when it took him three weekends. He was a quiet man in a loud extended family, the second of four brothers, all of whom had strong opinions about football. Diego had no opinions about football. He had opinions about bridges, about the right way to mount a shelf, and about which taco truck on East 6th was underappreciated.

On the morning of February 2, he woke up early and put a pot of coffee on, then went to look at the weather. The forecast said 64 and sunny, which in February in central Texas meant a small gift. He drove to the job site, parked, stood outside for a minute with his coffee, and watched two hawks doing slow, lazy circles over the scaffold. He thought about calling his mother but decided to wait until lunch, because he was going to call his mother at lunch and he knew himself well enough not to pretend otherwise.

By the time he got home, he had solved a small problem with a rebar detail, eaten a decent torta, and texted his oldest brother a single, complete sentence. The reply was immediate and unrelated to the text, which is how his family had communicated for 35 years. He ate leftover rice, watched 20 minutes of a hockey game, and went to bed thinking about the small rebar detail a little more, the way he always did when he had solved something well. The hawks, he decided, were a sign of nothing in particular. He liked that they were that.
