A Reader Story — Sunday Coffee, February 9

Renee Holloway was 36, a pediatric nurse in Charlotte, and the mother of a five-year-old who had recently decided that breakfast was a meal worth discussing. Her son, Theo, was a serious child with serious opinions about toast. He had a friend at preschool named Iris. He had a stuffed elephant named Geoffrey. He had started to read small words on signs, which Renee found both delightful and slightly alarming. Time was doing its thing.

On a Sunday in early February, she and Theo had pancakes at the kitchen table, the kind with the slightly burnt edges she always meant to fix. He ate three. He had chocolate milk. He told her, in the way five-year-olds tell things, that he had decided to be a paleontologist, and then a chef, and then, possibly, a fireman. She said those were all good options. He said he would think about it.

After breakfast, they went to the park. The sky was a thin, unpromising grey, but Theo did not care. He ran. He climbed. He found a stick and showed it to her with the gravity of a person presenting a document. Renee sat on a bench and watched him and thought, with no particular urgency, that the year was fine. The pancakes were good. The stick was a good stick. The day was the right size, and so was he. By 4 o'clock they were home, and by 5 he was asleep on the couch, and by 6 she was reading a novel she had been neglecting, and the day, in other words, was the right size.
