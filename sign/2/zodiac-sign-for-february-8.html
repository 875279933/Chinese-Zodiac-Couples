A Aquarius Story — Nate, February 8

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Nate Lindquist was 30, a nurse in Detroit, and the friend who would recommend a podcast at 11 p.m. and then not text back for three days. They were unreasonably loyal -- opinionated about things nobody else cared about and weirdly helpful when something was actually wrong. Their apartment had a magnet from every state they've visited and a stack of books about a subject they had recently become, temporarily, obsessed with.

Later that afternoon, Nate sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 40: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Nate was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Nate was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 30. The Aquarius in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
