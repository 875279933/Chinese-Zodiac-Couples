Reader Profile — A Saturday in the Garden, February 28

A short, personal read on this day

Reader Portrait — A Saturday in the Garden, February 28

Gianna Russo was 49, a landscape architect in Charleston, and the kind of person who had started, in the last few years, to keep a small vegetable garden in her backyard. The garden was, in her professional view, an amateur garden. It was, in her personal opinion, the right kind of garden. She had tomatoes in summer, herbs year-round, and a small patch of okra that had done better than she had any right to expect last August. She had a husband, two teenage sons, a small house, and a brown dog who had recently decided that okra was, in fact, food.

On a Saturday in late February, she went out to the garden at 8, in the way she did on Saturdays, with coffee, and looked at the beds. The herbs were coming back. The tomato cages were, in her professional view, too rusty. She made a list, on the back of an envelope, of things to do. The list was short. The list was, in her view, the right size for a Saturday in February.

By the afternoon, she had pruned the rosemary, edged the beds, and sat on the back step for an hour with a novel she had been neglecting. Her husband came out at 4 with two glasses of iced tea. They sat. They did not, in fact, talk about the garden. They talked about their sons. They talked about a trip they were thinking of taking in May. By 5, the dog had come out and sat between them, which was the right place for a dog to be on a Saturday in February. The list, in her view, was a good list. The year was going to be fine.
