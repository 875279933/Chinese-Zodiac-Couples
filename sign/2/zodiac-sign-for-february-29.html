A short, personal read on this day

Reader Portrait — A Leap Day, February 29

Vivian Klein was 24, a graduate student in public health in Philadelphia, and the kind of person who had recently turned a corner on a project she had been stuck on for three months. She had a small apartment, a serious coffee habit, and a boyfriend who was a software engineer, who had, in her view, the right idea about most things. She had, in the last 18 months, become the kind of person who had a small group of friends and a long list of people she texted back too late.

On a Saturday in late February, which happened to be the 29th, she woke up, made coffee, and thought about what to do with a day that only came around every four years. She decided, after about 20 minutes, that she was going to do nothing, in particular. She was going to do, in fact, exactly what she would have done on a regular Saturday in February, which is to say she was going to be at home.

By the afternoon, she had gone to the bookstore, bought a novel, made a stew, and read for two hours. Her boyfriend came over at 6 with a bottle of wine. They ate. They watched a movie they had been meaning to watch. They did not, in fact, do anything special, which is, in her view, the right kind of leap day. The leap day, in her view, did not, in fact, need to be special. It needed to be a Saturday, and it was, and the stew was good, and the movie was good, and the year was going to be fine.
