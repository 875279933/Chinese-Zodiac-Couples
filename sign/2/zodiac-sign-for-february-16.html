A Reader Story — An Empty Calendar, February 16

Connor Bailey was 30, a freelance illustrator in Brooklyn, and the kind of person whose calendar had, in his view, too many blocks. He had spent the last year turning down jobs he did not want, which was harder than it sounded. He had a small studio in a large building, a cat named Lola, and a girlfriend who was a chef at a restaurant he could not afford to eat at, which was fine, because she sometimes brought things home.

On a Wednesday in mid-February, he had a calendar that was, for the first time in weeks, empty. This was not, technically, good news. But it was, he decided, a kind of news. He made coffee, sat at his desk, and looked at the empty calendar for a few minutes. Then he did what he had not done in a long time: he drew something for himself. It was not a project. It was not a brief. It was a drawing of a cat, his cat, sitting on a windowsill, looking at a bird that was not there.

By the afternoon, he had done two more drawings. None of them were any good, in his professional view, but they were, in his personal opinion, exactly right. His girlfriend came home at 7 with a container of something she had made at the restaurant, and they ate it on the floor of the studio with chopsticks. They did not talk about the calendar. They did not talk about the year. They talked about the cat, the bird that was not there, the drawing, and whether the container was, in fact, a tupperware. The day, in other words, was the right size. The drawings were not for anyone. That was the point.
