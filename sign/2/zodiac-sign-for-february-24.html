Field Notes — A Saturday Drive, February 24

Daniel Klein was 56, a civil engineer in Phoenix, and the kind of person who had started to take a long drive on Saturdays, with no particular destination, just to see what was out there. He had been doing this for a year. He had, in that time, driven past a lot of nothing, a few small towns, and a particularly good sandwich place about 90 minutes outside the city that he had been to four times. He had a wife who did not mind his being gone on Saturdays, partly because she had her own things to do, partly because he always came back with a sandwich.

On a Saturday in late February, he got up, made coffee, kissed his wife, and went. He drove south, then east, then a little north. He ended up at a state park he had not been to in 20 years. He walked for an hour. He ate the sandwich in the truck, with the engine off, looking at a canyon. He did not take a picture. He had decided, a year ago, that he was not going to take pictures on these drives. He was going to, in fact, just be there.

He got home at 5. His wife was reading. The dog was asleep on the couch. He sat down. He did not, in fact, have a sandwich for her, which was a small failure. She did not mind. They had dinner at 6:30. They watched a show they had been watching. By 9:30, he was in bed. The drive had been, in his view, the right kind of drive. The canyon had been, in his view, the right kind of canyon. The year was going to be fine.
