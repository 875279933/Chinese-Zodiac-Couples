Reader Portrait — A Slow Wednesday, February 27

Greta Lindstrom was 41, a high school guidance counselor in Boise, and the kind of person who had, after 14 years on the job, gotten good at recognizing the look that students get when they are about to say something important. She had a husband who was a software developer, a daughter in middle school, a small garden, and a recurring fantasy about a small house on the Oregon coast, which was, in her view, both achievable and not quite the right time.

On a Wednesday in late February, she had a quiet day at work. Two students had come in for 30-minute appointments, neither of them in crisis, both of them doing better than they had been. She ate lunch at her desk. She went home at 4. Her daughter was at a friend's house. Her husband was at work. The dog met her at the door with the usual enthusiasm. The kitchen was, in her view, a small mess, which was the right kind of mess for a Wednesday.

She made dinner. She cleaned the kitchen. She read for an hour. She called her sister in Portland, who had recently started a small candle business, which was, in her view, both interesting and a little scary. They talked for 45 minutes. By 9, the dog was asleep on the couch. By 10, she was in bed. The day, in her view, was the right size. The year, in her view, was going to be fine. The house on the coast would still be there next year.
