A Reader Story — A Small Errand, February 14

Imani Carter was 33, a wedding photographer in Atlanta, and recently the kind of person who had been to 11 weddings in 18 months. She had been to so many weddings that she had developed opinions about centerpieces. She had been in a relationship for two years, with a man who, like her, had been to a lot of weddings, and who was also, like her, in no particular rush. They had a dog named Pearl. They had good friends. They had, in her view, a good life.

On a Thursday in mid-February, which happened to be Valentine's Day, she woke up and did not, in fact, have to shoot a wedding, which is to say she had the day mostly to herself. Her boyfriend had a meeting at 9 and would not be free until evening. She made coffee, walked Pearl, and went to the small hardware store near her house, where she bought a new mailbox number, because hers had been fading for two years and she had been meaning to fix it for one.

She put the new number up by 10:30, then made a quiche she had been thinking about for a week, because the photo she had seen online looked promising. The quiche was, in the end, a 7 out of 10. She ate half of it for lunch and saved the other half for the boyfriend, who came home at 6 with a small bag of dark chocolate, which he knew she liked, and a card that was, in his words, mostly a joke. They ate the quiche. The quiche was a 7. The chocolate was an 8. The day, in other words, was the right size, even if it was February 14.
