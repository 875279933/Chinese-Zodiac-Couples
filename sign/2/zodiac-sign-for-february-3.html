A Reader Story — Plan B, February 3

Priya Ranganathan was 28, a data analyst in Chicago, and the friend who carried a small notebook for everyone else. She had a sister, a brother-in-law, two nieces who drew her pictures on a near-monthly basis, and a job she was not sure she wanted to keep. She had been in Chicago for four years and was starting to notice that her favorite coffee shop had changed its hours twice, which she took, irrationally, as a sign.

On the second Friday in February -- a real, unremarkable Friday -- she left work at 5, took the train home, and decided she was going to make dal. This was a plan, because Priya did not make dal on Fridays. Friday was for takeout, leftover pasta, or whatever her roommate had left in the fridge. But tonight she wanted the slow, low heat of a pot on the stove, the sound of cumin in oil, the small project of a real meal. So she did that.

By 8:30 the apartment smelled like a proper kitchen and her roommate, Daniel, had come home to the sound of a song he did not know, played too loud. He sat down at the counter without being asked and said, with some surprise, that this was good. She said, also with some surprise, that it was. They ate at the counter, talked about nothing in particular, and by 10 o'clock she was reading a paperback she had been neglecting. Outside, the snow was starting to come down in the way snow does in Chicago when it means it. She turned the page.
