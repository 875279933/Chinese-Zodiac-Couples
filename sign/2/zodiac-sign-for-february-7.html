A Reader Story — A Good Tuesday, February 7

Eleanor Park was 31, a veterinarian in Denver, and the kind of person who made eye contact with every dog she passed on the street. She had a small clinic of her own, two partners she trusted, and a cat at home named Stan who had the run of the apartment. She had been single for 18 months and had recently started to enjoy it, which had taken longer than she expected. She still got a little nervous on dating apps, which is to say she was a normal person of a normal age.

On a Tuesday in early February, a week that had no particular weight, she left work at a normal time, picked up groceries on the way home, and made a stew. The stew was not ambitious. It was the stew she always made, with the carrots cut the way she always cut them, in the pot her mother had given her. She ate it at the kitchen counter, standing, because Stan was looking at her chair and she did not want to negotiate.

She watched one episode of a show she was behind on, called her sister in Boulder, and went to bed at a reasonable hour. The next morning, the snow that had been forecast arrived. She shoveled her walk, drove to work in careful 20-minute increments, and pulled into the clinic lot 5 minutes early. The day, like the stew, was not ambitious. It was, however, the right size. She thought, as she often did on good Tuesdays, that the year was going to be fine. She did not have to do anything about it. It was just going to be fine.
