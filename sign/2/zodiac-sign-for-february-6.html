A Reader Story — Snow and Salt, February 6

Patrick Whelan was 62, a retired mail carrier in Buffalo, and a man who had lived through enough Februarys to be unimpressed by snow. He had delivered mail for 34 years, mostly in the same neighborhood, and he knew every dog by name and which ones were bluffers. His knees were bad. His back was worse. He had a wife who made a serious pot of soup every Sunday and a granddaughter who had recently started to send him actual letters in the mail, on paper, with stickers.

On a Monday in early February, he got up at 5:45, looked out the window, and decided the driveway needed to be done before coffee. This was a small rebellion. The rule in the Whelan house, set by his wife Nora, was that coffee came first. He did the driveway anyway, came in cold, hands red, and sat down with a mug and a small, private feeling of satisfaction. Nora did not say anything, which was its own kind of approval.

By the afternoon, the snowplow had come through twice and buried the driveway again. He did not go out. He watched the hockey game with the volume low, ate a roast beef sandwich his wife had made, and read his granddaughter's letter for the second time. It was about a turtle she had at school named Mr. Pickle. He thought Mr. Pickle was an excellent name. He thought February was fine. He thought, as he did on most Mondays, that he had lived a good enough life to be sitting in this chair.
