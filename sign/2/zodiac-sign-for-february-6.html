A Aquarius Story — Ethan, February 6

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Ethan Vogel was 45, a photographer in Raleigh, and the friend who would recommend a podcast at 11 p.m. and then not text back for three days. They were quietly stubborn -- opinionated about things nobody else cared about and weirdly helpful when something was actually wrong. Their apartment had a magnet from every state they've visited and a stack of books about a subject they had recently become, temporarily, obsessed with.

On their birthday, Ethan got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Ethan ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

Before falling asleep, Ethan wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On February 6, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Ethan thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
