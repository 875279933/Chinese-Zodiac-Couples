A Aquarius Story — Sage, February 4

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Sage Bennett was 38, a musician in Milwaukee, and the friend who would recommend a podcast at 11 p.m. and then not text back for three days. They were the friend who brings the snacks -- opinionated about things nobody else cared about and weirdly helpful when something was actually wrong. Their apartment had a recipe box full of handwritten cards from their mom and a stack of books about a subject they had recently become, temporarily, obsessed with.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Sage had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Sage yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

Before falling asleep, Sage wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On February 4, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Sage thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
