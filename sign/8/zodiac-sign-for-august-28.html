A Virgo Story — Luna, August 28

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Luna Nelson was 38, worked as a elementary school teacher in Seattle, and maintained a color-coded Google Calendar so specific that even their dentist commented on it. They were a creature of habit -- the friend who would proofread your resume, tell you the jacket was not the one, and then drive you to the interview anyway. Their apartment had a folded blanket on the back of the couch, because that was the kind of thing a Virgo would notice and fix.

On their birthday, Luna got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Luna ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Luna was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 38. The Virgo in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
