A Leo Story — Oscar, August 10

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Oscar Lindquist was 39, a high school coach in St. Louis, and the unofficial social secretary of a friend group that had somehow stayed intact since college. They were secretly funny and the first person anyone called when they needed a speech, a toast, or someone to tell the waiter it was, in fact, their birthday. Oscar genuinely did not care about being the center of attention, which is exactly why the center of attention kept finding them.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Oscar had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Oscar yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Oscar was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 39. The Leo in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
