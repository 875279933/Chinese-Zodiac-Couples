A Libra Story — Gavin, October 18

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Gavin Patterson was 32, a veterinary technician in Chicago, and the only person in their friend group who could pick a restaurant without drama. They were quietly competitive, and their apartment had lighting so intentional that people always mentioned it. Gavin had a framed photo from a 2012 trip, because a Libra without small, nice things is basically just a person with an opinion.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Gavin spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 18, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Gavin was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 32. The Libra in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
