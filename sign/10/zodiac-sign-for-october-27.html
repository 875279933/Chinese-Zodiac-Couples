A Scorpio Story — Cyrus Montague, October 27

A short, personal read on this day

Cyrus Montague was 43, a small-press novelist in Ann Arbor. They had a dim apartment light they refused to replace because, as far as they were concerned, the right light was worth waiting for. They had a long-standing habit of leaving voice messages instead of texts, mostly because they didn't trust themselves to keep things short in writing. Their friends had, on more than one occasion, described them as 'a lot' and meant it in a way that was, on balance, affectionate.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Cyrus spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 27, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Cyrus got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow was a different kind of day, and that was fine.
