A Libra Story — Bryan Wojciechowski, October 13

A small, personal read on this day

Bryan Wojciechowski was 46, a sheet metal worker in Buffalo. They kept a small notebook of things they noticed in their own handwriting, and they read it on slow afternoons. Most entries were short. They made a point of keeping certain friendships close without ever saying so, and they had, in their lifetime, returned two borrowed sweaters and forgotten about four.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Bryan spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 13, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Bryan got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow was a different kind of day, and that was fine.
