A Libra Story — Theo Whitlock, October 20

A short, personal read on this day

Theo Whitlock was 31, a stand-up comedian in Portland. They owned a cast-iron pan that they had seasoned, over years, into something close to a member of the family. They walked with a slight forward lean, the kind that suggested they were always heading toward the next thing. They had a way of asking follow-up questions that, depending on the day, felt like a kindness or a job interview.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Theo spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 20, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Theo got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow was a different kind of day, and that was fine.
