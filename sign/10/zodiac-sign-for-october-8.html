A Libra Story — Camila Ostrowski, October 8

A quick read on a real-feeling day

Camila Ostrowski was 24, a junior copywriter in Chicago. They owned one good jacket and four perfectly average ones. They ran every morning in a neighborhood that didn't really need running in, but they had started the habit and refused to break it. Most days, they had a single, useful thought and at least three that they couldn't find a place for.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Camila spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 8, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Camila got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow was a different kind of day, and that was fine.
