A Scorpio Story — Halsey Pemberton, October 31

A short, personal read on this day

Halsey Pemberton was 34, a tax attorney in Atlanta. They kept a running list of small things they would fix, someday, in no particular order. The list was long. They had a habit of going to bed at a reasonable hour and a habit of staying up too late, and they were at peace with the contradiction. Most days, they had one good idea and a small stack of average ones.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Halsey spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on October 31, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Halsey got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow was a different kind of day, and that was fine.
