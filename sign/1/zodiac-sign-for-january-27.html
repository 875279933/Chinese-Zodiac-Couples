Zodiac Sign Vignette

Parker Ellis was 47, a middle school science teacher in Atlanta, and the kind of colleague who could fix a classroom projector without swearing. They were enthusiastic in a way that had survived 20 years of teaching sixth graders, and quietly funny in a way their students occasionally noticed. Their classroom had a tarantula named Dr. Henderson and a periodic table that had started peeling in 2014.

On a Monday in January, Parker taught a lesson about sound. They rang a tuning fork, set a bowl of water on the demo table, and watched 28 kids lean in. One student asked why the water jumped. Parker did the obvious thing and said, "Because the air is pushing it, and air is mostly just us trying not to fall over." The student nodded. Parker marked this as a good Monday.

By the time Parker got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, sits in a different sign.
