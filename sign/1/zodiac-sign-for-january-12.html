A January 12 Reader Story

A short story about someone born on this day

Profile Vignette

Lucas Ashford was 40, a small-business owner in Baltimore, and the person friends called when they needed a real decision made. They were the friend who brings the snacks -- steady, reliable, and got funnier the longer you knew them. Lucas kept a paper planner. The phone version, in their view, did not hold up.

On their birthday, Lucas got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Lucas ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

Before falling asleep, Lucas wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On January 12, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Lucas thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
