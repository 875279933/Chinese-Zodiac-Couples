A January 6 Reader Story

A short story about someone born on this day

Emma Hollis was 41, a library administrator in Nashville, and the person friends called when they needed a real decision made. They were a little too honest -- steady, reliable, and got funnier the longer you knew them. Emma kept a paper planner. The phone version, in their view, did not hold up.

On their birthday, Emma got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Emma ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

When the phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Emma found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and did not delete any. Being a Capricorn, Emma thought, was less about the loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
