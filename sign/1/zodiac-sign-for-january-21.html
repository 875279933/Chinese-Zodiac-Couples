A Aquarius Story — Ruby, January 21

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Ruby Quinlan was 28, a pastry chef in Salt Lake City, and the friend who would recommend a podcast at 11 p.m. and then not text back for three days. They were famous for a story they don't like to tell -- opinionated about things nobody else cared about and weirdly helpful when something was actually wrong. Their apartment had a kettle that is almost always on and a stack of books about a subject they had recently become, temporarily, obsessed with.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Ruby had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Ruby yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

By the time Ruby got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, is a different sign entirely.
