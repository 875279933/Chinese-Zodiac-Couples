A January 9 Reader Story

A short story about someone born on this day

Ryan Patterson was 29, a software engineer in Austin, and the person friends called when they needed a real decision made. They were secretly funny -- steady, reliable, and got funnier the longer you knew them. Ryan kept a paper planner. The phone version, in their view, did not hold up.

Later that afternoon, Ryan sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 39: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Ryan was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

Before falling asleep, Ryan wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On January 9, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Ryan thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
