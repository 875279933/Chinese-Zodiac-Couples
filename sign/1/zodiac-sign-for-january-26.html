A Aquarius Story — Ryan, January 26

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Ryan Whitaker was 46, a bartender in Detroit, and the friend who would recommend a podcast at 11 p.m. and then not text back for three days. They were weirdly good with directions -- opinionated about things nobody else cared about and weirdly helpful when something was actually wrong. Their apartment had a framed photo from a 2012 trip and a stack of books about a subject they had recently become, temporarily, obsessed with.

Later that afternoon, Ryan sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 56: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Ryan was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Ryan found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Aquarius, Ryan thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
