A Cancer Story — Devin, July 16

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Devin Underwood was 35, a dentist from Portland, and the kind of person who still had a window sill full of plants, some thriving and some clearly struggling from their childhood home. They were quietly stubborn -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Devin kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

Later that afternoon, Devin sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 45: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Devin was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Devin was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 35. The Cancer in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
