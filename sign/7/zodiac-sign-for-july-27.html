A Leo Story — Maya, July 27

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Maya DAmico was 30, a architect in Nashville, and the unofficial social secretary of a friend group that had somehow stayed intact since college. They were famous among their friends and the first person anyone called when they needed a speech, a toast, or someone to tell the waiter it was, in fact, their birthday. Maya genuinely did not care about being the center of attention, which is exactly why the center of attention kept finding them.

Later that afternoon, Maya sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 40: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Maya was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Maya found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Leo, Maya thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
