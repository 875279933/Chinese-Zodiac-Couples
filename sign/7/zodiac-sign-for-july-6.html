A Cancer Story — Wyatt, July 6

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Wyatt Jeffries was 43, a nurse from Phoenix, and the kind of person who still had a folded blanket on the back of the couch from their childhood home. They were unexpectedly romantic -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Wyatt kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Wyatt had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Wyatt yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Wyatt found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Cancer, Wyatt thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
