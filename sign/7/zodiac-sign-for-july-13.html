A Cancer Story — Camille, July 13

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Camille Fernandez was 46, a architect from Phoenix, and the kind of person who still had a magnet from every state they've visited from their childhood home. They were a creature of habit -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. Camille kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

On their birthday, Camille got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Camille ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

Before falling asleep, Camille wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On July 13, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Camille thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
