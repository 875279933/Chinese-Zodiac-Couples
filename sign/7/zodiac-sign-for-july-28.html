A Leo Story — Hadley, July 28

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Hadley Vogel was 33, a software engineer in St. Louis, and the unofficial social secretary of a friend group that had somehow stayed intact since college. They were the friend who brings the snacks and the first person anyone called when they needed a speech, a toast, or someone to tell the waiter it was, in fact, their birthday. Hadley genuinely did not care about being the center of attention, which is exactly why the center of attention kept finding them.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Hadley had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Hadley yelled back, which is, in their opinion, how birthdays are supposed to go.

Before falling asleep, Hadley wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On July 28, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Hadley thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
