A Cancer Story — June, July 3

A short read about someone born on this exact day

June Yarborough was 32, a editor from Pittsburgh, and the kind of person who still had a framed photo from a 2012 trip from their childhood home. They were terrible at texting back -- the friend who showed up with soup when you didn't ask for it and remembered to text your mom on her birthday. June kept a drawer full of things people had left at their place over the years, because you never knew when someone would want their hat back.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and June spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on July 3, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time June got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, is a different sign entirely.
