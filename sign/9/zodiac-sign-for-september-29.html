A Libra Story — Parker, September 29

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Parker Ashford was 45, a chef in Brooklyn, and the only person in their friend group who could pick a restaurant without drama. They were a creature of habit, and their apartment had lighting so intentional that people always mentioned it. Parker had a magnet from every state they've visited, because a Libra without small, nice things is basically just a person with an opinion.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Parker spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on September 29, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

By the time Parker got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, is a different sign entirely.
