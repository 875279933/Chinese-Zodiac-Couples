A Libra Story — Ava, September 25

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Ava Turnbull was 31, a pastry chef in New Orleans, and the only person in their friend group who could pick a restaurant without drama. They were unreasonably loyal, and their apartment had lighting so intentional that people always mentioned it. Ava had a folded blanket on the back of the couch, because a Libra without small, nice things is basically just a person with an opinion.

Later that afternoon, Ava sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 41: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Ava was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

By the time Ava got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47, which is a fine time for an adult to go to bed and nobody gets to say otherwise. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, is a different sign entirely.
