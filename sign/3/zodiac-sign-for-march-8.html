A Pisces Story — Marcus, March 8

A short story about someone born on this day, kept deliberately unsentimental

Marcus Delgado was 34, a bookstore manager in Chicago, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were quietly competitive — the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Marcus kept a window sill full of plants, some thriving and some clearly struggling because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

Later that afternoon, Marcus sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 44: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Marcus was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

By the time Marcus got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, sits in a different sign.
