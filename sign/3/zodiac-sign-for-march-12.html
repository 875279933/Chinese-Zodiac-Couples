A Pisces Story — Luna, March 12

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Luna Nelson was 26, a high school coach in Minneapolis, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were the friend who brings the snacks -- the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Luna kept a kettle that is almost always on because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to 'have a good one,' and Luna spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on March 12, were not among them. This felt, in its own quiet way, like progress.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Luna found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Pisces, Luna thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
