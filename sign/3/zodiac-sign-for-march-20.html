A Pisces Story — Maya, March 20

A short story about someone born on this day, kept deliberately unsentimental

Maya Everett was 33, a photographer in Portland, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were unexpectedly romantic — the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Maya kept a framed photo from a 2012 trip because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Maya had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Maya yelled back, which is, in their view, how birthdays go.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Maya was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 33. The Pisces in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
