A Pisces Story — Sage, March 13

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Sage Iverson was 30, a pastry chef in Richmond, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were unexpectedly romantic -- the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Sage kept a kettle that is almost always on because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

Later that afternoon, Sage sat in their car in a grocery store parking lot for 17 minutes and thought about nothing in particular. Sometimes a person just needs 17 minutes. They had a running list of things they wanted to do before they were 40: learn to bake sourdough, visit the coast, find that one jacket they lost in 2017, call their dad more. The list was mostly aspirational and, as far as Sage was concerned, that was the correct way for a list to be.

When their phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Sage found a charger -- eventually -- and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, stored in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and deleted exactly zero. Being a Pisces, Sage thought, was less about the big, loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.
