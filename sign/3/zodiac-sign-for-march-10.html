A Pisces Story — Daniel, March 10

A short story about someone born on this day, kept deliberately unsentimental

Daniel Yarborough was 41, a chef in Phoenix, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were terrible at texting back — the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Daniel kept a record player that gets used more than any appliance because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

On their birthday, Daniel got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate — the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years — left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Daniel ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

By the time Daniel got home that evening, their phone had 11 texts, one missed call from their sister, and a spam email they opened by accident. They ate leftovers for dinner, watched one episode of something they had already seen, and went to bed at 10:47. Before they turned the light off, they thought about the year ahead: a trip they might take, a conversation they had been putting off, a playlist they still hadn't finished. Then they turned the light off. Tomorrow, after all, sits in a different sign.

Field Note

Pisces is the sign that remembers what everyone else at the party has already forgotten. The playlist, the look on someone's face in 2014, the specific way a friend takes their coffee. That kind of memory is a small, useful kind of magic.
