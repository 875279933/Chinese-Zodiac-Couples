A Pisces Story — Leah, March 14

A short story about someone born on this day, kept deliberately unsentimental

Leah Delgado was 33, a freelance writer in Pittsburgh, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were quietly stubborn — the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Leah kept a kettle that is almost always on because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

Their closest friend had been trying to plan a surprise for three weeks, and Leah had been quietly pretending not to know about it for 20 days. They liked surprises in theory and hated them in practice, which is why they had, very gently, texted their friend the exact outfit they were planning to wear. Not to control things, they told themselves. Just to make the pictures turn out. When they walked in the door, everyone yelled. Leah yelled back, which is, in their view, how birthdays go.

When the phone died at 9:30 p.m., instead of panicking, Leah found a charger — eventually — and spent the rest of the night looking at old pictures. Not the ones on Instagram. The ones from 2014, in a folder nobody opens anymore. They smiled at a few, laughed at one, and did not delete any. Being a Pisces, Leah thought, was less about the loud traits everyone talked about and more about the small, quiet ones: the things you save, the people you call first, the night you stay home and feel, for 15 very specific minutes, completely caught up.

Field Note

Pisces is the sign that remembers what everyone else at the party has already forgotten. The playlist, the look on someone's face in 2014, the specific way a friend takes their coffee. That kind of memory is a small, useful kind of magic.
