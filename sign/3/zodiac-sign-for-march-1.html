A Pisces Story — Lucas, March 1

A short story about someone born on this day, kept deliberately unsentimental

Lucas Hartley was 31, a software engineer in Brooklyn, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were quietly competitive — the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Lucas kept a recipe box full of handwritten cards from their mom because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

A stranger at the coffee shop told them to "have a good one," and Lucas spent the next hour wondering why that phrase was so much better than most things people actually say. They worked through lunch, walked around the block afterward, and had a very honest conversation with themselves about whether they could pull off wearing a hat. They decided, eventually, that some people are just hat people and that they, on March 1, were not among them. It felt, in its own way, like progress.

They told themselves they would take the day off next year, which is what they told themselves every year, and which they had never once actually done. Lucas was, for better and for worse, the kind of person who showed up. Who checked the work email on a Sunday. Who answered the phone when their mom called, even when they were in the middle of something. It was, they thought, probably fine. They were 31. The Pisces in them knew exactly how much of the plan to stick to and exactly how much to wing.
