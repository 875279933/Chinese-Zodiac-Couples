A Pisces Story — Clara, March 15

A short read about someone born on this exact day

Clara Hollis was 37, a bookstore manager in Chicago, and the kind of person who would cry at a dog food commercial and then pretend they didn't. They were surprisingly patient -- the friend who always had a playlist for the mood you didn't know you were in. Clara kept a stack of library books on the nightstand because their aunt had given it to them when they were 12, and that was, honestly, reason enough.

On their birthday, Clara got up early, made coffee, and stood at the window for about 10 minutes doing absolutely nothing. This was a ritual, even though they would never call it that out loud. Their roommate -- the one who had been in and out of four different jobs in two years -- left a cupcake on the counter with a single, slightly misspelled note. Clara ate it standing up and got to work 12 minutes early, because 12 is a good round number and because that's just the kind of person they were.

Before falling asleep, Clara wrote down one thing in a notebook they kept by the bed. They had been doing this, off and on, since they were 19. Sometimes the thing was big. Usually it was small. On March 15, it was small: a good sandwich, an honest compliment from a coworker, a text from an old friend they had been meaning to call. That was the thing about birthdays, Clara thought. You expect a big moment and you usually end up with three or four small ones that, eventually, turn out to be the point.
