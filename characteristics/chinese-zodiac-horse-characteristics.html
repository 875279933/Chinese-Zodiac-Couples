The Horse profile runs on movement. Variety beats routine, change beats stability, and the next challenge beats the current one. Energy is high, and the sign operates well across new contexts: a new city, a new team, a new project. People read the Horse as upbeat, easy to talk to, and quick to adapt when the plan changes midstream.

Independence is the second pillar. The Horse values the freedom to choose, and roles that lock in the calendar too tightly drain the sign. Travel, varied work, and roles that mix people and contexts fit better than narrow, repetitive ones. Social energy runs high: a Horse usually knows more people in the room than anyone else, and the network grows fast.

The watch points: leaving projects half-finished when the next one calls, and saying yes to too many commitments. The same traits that produce the Horse profile (pace, range, social fluency) also produce restlessness when the work plateaus. The fix is not less motion. The fix is better project selection, clearer finish lines, and a calendar that earns its pace.
