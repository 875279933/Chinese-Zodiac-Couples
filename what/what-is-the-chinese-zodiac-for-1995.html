What is the Chinese zodiac for 1995?

The 1995 Chinese zodiac is the Wood Pig. If you were born in 1995, your zodiac sign combines the Pig's kindness with the warmth of the Wood element.

The 1995 Wood Pig: Kind & Generous

Ever wondered what is the Chinese zodiac for 1995? Let me spill—it's the Year of the Wood Pig! If your birthday falls between January 31, 1995 and February 18, 1996, you're one of these kind-hearted souls. Wood Pigs are like that friend who always sees the bright side—compassionate, positive, and totally generous with their time and love. The Pig is the twelfth and final animal in the zodiac cycle, known for kindness, generosity, and loyalty. Add Wood energy, and you get someone who's not just caring—they're optimistic, warm, and always looking to grow and spread positivity. These are the friends who'll organize a surprise party for you or share their last slice of pizza without hesitation!

The True Nature of Wood Pigs

What really makes 1995 Wood Pigs tick? It's a blend of Pig kindness and Wood warmth.

Wood Pigs are the people who make the room feel softer. Regular Pigs are kind, generous, the kind of person who shares the last slice without thinking about it. Wood Pigs take all of that and add a layer underneath. They have the same warmth, the same instinct to take care of the people they love, the same way of making an ordinary afternoon feel a little more like home—but they also bring the growth, the optimism, the kind of energy that makes the people around them feel like things are going to be okay. People born in 1995 have a kind of presence that makes the people around them feel a little lighter.

What sets Wood Pigs apart is the way they mix kindness with follow-through. They don't just notice the friend who needs help—they actually show up, and they keep showing up. They're the one who remembers the off-hand mention from three months ago, the one who brings the meal when someone has a baby, the one who makes the ordinary holiday feel like something to remember. They have a real gift for making the people they love feel like the people they love are actually lucky.

The flip side? Wood Pigs give too much, and they don't always know when to stop. They can be a little too trusting, a little too quick to forgive, a little too ready to put the people they love ahead of themselves. They can carry the weight of other people's problems and call it love. The real growth for a Wood Pig is learning that taking care of yourself isn't the same as being selfish—and that the people who love you would rather see you well than see you give it all away.

Wait—Am I Actually a Wood Pig?

Here's a common point of confusion: the Chinese zodiac follows the lunar calendar, not the Gregorian one. The 1995 Wood Pig year starts on January 31, 1995. So if you were born in early January 1995, you're actually a Wood Dog from 1994, not a Wood Pig.

This matters! A lot of people born in early January get their zodiac sign wrong. Always double-check your exact birth date to know for sure what is your Chinese zodiac sign.

What Happened in 1995

A few of the moments Americans still talk about from the year the Wood Pigs were born.

April
The Oklahoma City Bombing
On April 19, 1995, a truck bomb destroyed the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City. 168 people were killed—the deadliest terror attack on U.S. soil until 9/11.
June
"Toy Story" Lands in Theaters
Pixar's Toy Story opened June 22, 1995—the first fully computer-animated feature film ever released, and the movie that made 1995 babies believe toys had a life of their own.
July
The eBay Auction Site Goes Live
Pierre Omidyar launched eBay in September 1995, but the summer was when the first weird items started showing up in the first online auctions—a whole new kind of American weekend.
September
O.J. Simpson Acquitted
On October 3, 1995, the O.J. Simpson verdict came down—not guilty—and the country split in half in a way the cable news shows kept replaying for years.
December
The Dayton Accords End the Bosnian War
The peace deal was signed in Paris on December 14, 1995, ending the war in Bosnia. It was the foreign-policy story American parents talked about at the kitchen table that year.
