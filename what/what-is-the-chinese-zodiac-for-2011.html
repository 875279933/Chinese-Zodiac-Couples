So, what is the Chinese zodiac for 2011? It's the Year of the Metal Rabbit! If your birthday falls between February 3, 2011 and January 22, 2012, you belong to one of the most quietly impressive signs in the zodiac. Metal Rabbits are the kind of people others immediately trust—calm, polite, and somehow always a step ahead.

Rabbits are known for being thoughtful, gentle, and elegant. They care about how things look and how people feel. Add Metal to the mix and you get someone with extra clarity and a sharper edge. Metal Rabbits don't just seem put-together—they really are.

Section OneThe True Nature of Metal Rabbits

What really drives 2011 Metal Rabbits? It's Rabbit kindness sharpened by a Metal mindset.

Metal Rabbits are easy to underestimate—and that's exactly how they like it. Rabbits on their own are warm, considerate, and natural peacemakers. They pick up on the mood in a room before anyone says a word, and they adjust accordingly. The Metal element turns that emotional intelligence into something more focused. Metal Rabbits read people well, but they also act on what they notice.

They have a way of making the people around them feel seen. Metal Rabbits remember birthdays, pick up on small shifts in tone, and show up with exactly what you needed—even if you didn't know you needed it. That kind of attentiveness makes them excellent friends, partners, and coworkers.

The flip side? Metal Rabbits sometimes overthink. They weigh every word, every decision, every possible outcome—and that can slow them down or push them toward people-pleasing. The growth edge for a Metal Rabbit is learning to say no, speak up, and trust that not everyone needs to like them.

February 3, 2011

The lunar new year ushered in the Metal Rabbit—a sign tied to grace, sensitivity, and careful progress. Festivities lit up Chinatowns from Singapore to San Francisco, with families gathering to mark a year that promised steady growth over loud breakthroughs.

A 9.0-magnitude undersea quake off the coast of Japan triggered a massive tsunami that killed nearly 20,000 people and crippled the Fukushima nuclear plant. It became one of the costliest natural disasters in history and reshaped Japan's energy policy.

U.S. Navy SEALs killed Osama bin Laden during a raid on his compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan. The operation closed a chapter that began on September 11, 2001, and dominated American foreign policy for a decade.
July 9, 2011
South Sudan Declares Independence
South Sudan officially became the world's newest country after decades of civil war. The split was celebrated as a moment of self-determination—and watched closely as a test of whether peace could hold in one of Africa's most volatile regions.
July 22, 2011
Norway Attacks
A lone attacker set off a car bomb in Oslo and then opened fire at a youth camp on Utøya island, killing 77 people. The tragedy shocked Norway and sparked a national reckoning about extremism, mental health, and political violence.
