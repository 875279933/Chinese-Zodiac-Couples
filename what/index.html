What is the Chinese Zodiac?

The Chinese zodiac is one of the oldest cultural traditions still in regular use. This page walks through how the twelve animal signs work, the role of the five elements, and how to find the sign tied to a given birth year.

What is the Chinese Zodiac?

What is the Chinese zodiac, in plain terms? It is a classification system that pairs each year on the lunar calendar with one of twelve animal signs. The pattern has been in use for over two thousand years and still shows up in birthday cards, family conversations, and calendar prints across East Asia.

The system has two moving parts. The first is the twelve-year animal cycle, where each year is assigned one of twelve animals in a fixed order: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. The second part is the five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water), which rotate on a two-year cycle. Combined, the two cycles stretch the full repetition to sixty years, so each animal-element pairing shows up only once in a typical lifetime.

One practical point that catches most newcomers off guard: the Chinese zodiac year does not start on January 1. It begins on Chinese New Year, which lands between January 21 and February 20 on the Gregorian calendar. People born in January or early February may belong to the previous year's sign, and the complete year index notes the lunar start date for each year. For a closer look at the twelve animal profiles, the zodiac sign pages cover each one in detail.

The same animal can read quite differently across decades because the element shifts the tone. Two Wood Rats share the same backbone but show up in different colors. The personality pages break that interaction down further.

The Five Elements

Each year in the cycle is paired with one of the five elements. The element shifts the tone of the animal, which is why two people born in the same sign but in different decades often feel quite different.

Wood

Wood leans into growth, creativity, and steady expansion. People with Wood in their year tend to be flexible, idea-driven, and good at working with a team.

Fire

Fire brings energy and directness. People with Fire in their year tend to act on instinct, speak openly, and stand out in a group without trying to.

Earth

Earth is steady and grounded. People with Earth in their year tend to be dependable, patient, and the kind of friends others call on when things get complicated.

Metal

Metal carries focus and discipline. People with Metal in their year tend to set clear goals and work toward them without much distraction.

Water

Water is reflective and adaptable. People with Water in their year tend to read situations quickly and adjust their approach without losing their own center.

What is the Chinese Zodiac - Common Questions

Short answers to the questions readers bring to this page most often.

What is the Chinese zodiac, in one sentence?

It is a classification system that pairs each year on the lunar calendar with one of twelve animal signs, on a cycle that repeats every twelve years and stretches to sixty years once the five elements are added.

How is the Chinese zodiac different from the Western zodiac?

The Western zodiac runs on twelve monthly signs based on the position of the sun. The Chinese zodiac runs on twelve yearly signs based on the lunar calendar, with a five-element overlay that extends the full cycle to sixty years.

What are the 12 Chinese zodiac animals in order?

Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. Each one takes its year in this fixed sequence before the cycle restarts.

When does the Chinese zodiac year start?

It starts on Chinese New Year, between January 21 and February 20 on the Gregorian calendar. The exact date shifts each year. People born in January or early February may belong to the previous year's sign on the lunar calendar.

What does my Chinese zodiac sign say about me?

Each animal sign has a set of tendencies that show up in personality reads. The element layered on top (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) adds a second layer. For a year-specific read, see the year of the Chinese zodiac directory.

Does the Chinese zodiac predict the future?

It is used as a framework for reading personality and relationships, not as a forecasting tool. Treat the animal and element descriptions as a starting point for self-reflection rather than predictions of specific events.
