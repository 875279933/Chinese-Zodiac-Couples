2021 Metal Oxen: Steadfast, Strong, and Unshakable

So, what is the Chinese zodiac for 2021? It's the Year of the Metal Ox! If your birthday falls between February 12, 2021 and January 31, 2022, you belong to one of the most dependable and hard-working signs in the zodiac. Metal Oxen are the kind of people who finish what they start—no matter what.

Oxen are known for their patience, discipline, and quiet strength. They don't need to be loud to be heard. They put their head down, they do the work, and they show up every time. Add Metal to the mix and you get an Ox that's even more resolute, more structured, and more determined. Metal Oxen don't flinch when things get hard—they just work harder.

Section Three

2021: A Year That Shaped the World

Five moments that defined the Year of the Metal Ox.

February 12, 2021
Year of the Metal Ox Begins
The lunar new year ushered in the Metal Ox, with fireworks and family gatherings held largely over Zoom for the second year running. The Ox is associated with hard work and steady progress—fitting for a year focused on rebuilding.
January 20, 2021
Inauguration Day Ceremony Held
The U.S. presidential inauguration took place at the Capitol under heavy security and a reduced crowd due to the pandemic. The ceremony featured a virtual parade and a primetime television broadcast with celebrity performances.
March 23, 2021
Suez Canal Blocked
The massive container ship Ever Given ran aground in the Suez Canal, blocking one of the world's busiest shipping lanes for six days. The incident disrupted an estimated $9.6 billion of global trade per day and showed how fragile global supply chains had become.
July 23, 2021
Tokyo Olympics Open
The 2020 Summer Olympics finally opened in Tokyo, a year late due to the pandemic. Held largely without spectators, the Games featured skateboarding, surfing, and sport climbing for the first time, and saw Simone Biles prioritize mental health mid-competition.
December 9, 2021
James Webb Telescope Launches
NASA launched the James Webb Space Telescope aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana. The $10 billion observatory, the most powerful ever built, is designed to study the first galaxies that formed after the Big Bang and to image exoplanet atmospheres in detail.
