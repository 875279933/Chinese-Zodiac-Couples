What is the Chinese zodiac for 2017?

If you were born in 2017, your Chinese zodiac sign is the Fire Rooster—a bold, organized mix of confidence and heart.

2017 Fire Roosters: Decisive, Honest, and Always on Point

So, what is the Chinese zodiac for 2017? It's the Year of the Fire Rooster! If your birthday falls between January 28, 2017 and February 15, 2018, you belong to one of the most dependable and outspoken signs in the zodiac. Fire Roosters are the kind of people you call when something needs to get done right.

Roosters are known for being confident, organized, and direct. They say what they think, they show up on time, and they hold themselves to a high standard. Add Fire to the mix and you get a Rooster with extra spark—more passion, more decisiveness, more drive. Fire Roosters don't just get things done; they get them done with style.

The True Nature of Fire Roosters

What really drives 2017 Fire Roosters? It's Rooster honesty with Fire fire.

Fire Roosters are the kind of people who make things happen. They wake up with a plan and they stick to it. Roosters on their own are already sharp, punctual, and proud of their work. They care about doing things well and they don't cut corners. The Fire element adds heat to that focus. Fire Roosters don't just want to do things right—they want to do them big, with passion, and on their terms.

They're honest to a fault. Fire Roosters say what they mean, even when it's not what people want to hear. Sometimes that directness stings—but it's part of why people trust them. With a Fire Rooster, you always know where you stand.

The challenge? Fire Roosters can be too hard on themselves and on others. They hold everyone to the same high bar, and they can be critical when things don't measure up. The growth edge is learning that not everyone operates the same way, and that being right isn't always the same as being kind.

Boldly Decisive

Fire Roosters don't sit on the fence. They make a call and they stick to it. That decisiveness makes them natural leaders in moments when the rest of the room is hesitating.

Dependable to the Core

Fire Roosters follow through. If they say they'll do it, they'll do it. People rely on them for that consistency, and they take that responsibility seriously.

Brutally Honest

Fire Roosters don't sugarcoat. They tell the truth even when it's uncomfortable, and they expect the same from the people around them. That kind of honesty is rare—and refreshing.

Sharp Organizers

Fire Roosters run a tight ship. They make lists, they plan ahead, they keep things in order. They're the friend who remembers the reservation and gets everyone out the door on time.

Quietly Confident

Fire Roosters know what they're good at. They don't need applause or validation—they get satisfaction from a job well done. That self-possession is part of what makes them so magnetic.

Strongly Driven

Fire Roosters don't coast. They set goals, they work hard, and they expect the same from the people around them. The Fire element turns that drive into something inspiring rather than just intense.

Love, Work & Money

How Fire Rooster traits shape the way 2017 babies handle relationships, careers, and finances.

Fire Roosters bring their focused, dependable energy into every part of life. In love, they're loyal and direct—but they need a partner who can handle the honesty. At work, they thrive in roles with clear goals and accountability. With money, they're disciplined and steady. The only real risk? They sometimes hold themselves and others to standards that aren't realistic, which can wear people out.

In Relationships

Fire Roosters are loyal, devoted, and refreshingly direct. They match well with Oxen, Snakes, and Dragons—signs that respect their drive and aren't afraid of honesty. They can find Rabbits and Pigs a little too soft for their pace, but patience and understanding can bridge that.

At Work

Fire Roosters thrive in roles with structure and accountability—management, finance, law, medicine, entrepreneurship, military, project management, operations. They hate slack environments and they hate being unclear on expectations. They do their best work when the bar is high and the goal is clear.

With Money

Fire Roosters are careful with cash. They budget, they save, and they don't make impulse buys. The Fire element gives them the confidence to invest when it makes sense—they just don't gamble with what they can't afford to lose.

The Balance

Fire Roosters thrive when they ease up a little. Not every detail is worth the stress, and not everyone is at the same level they're at. Lowering the bar occasionally isn't giving up—it's choosing peace.

Wait—Am I Actually a Fire Rooster?

Here's a common point of confusion: the Chinese zodiac follows the lunar calendar, not the Gregorian one. So if you were born before January 28, 2017, you're actually a Fire Monkey from 2016, not a Fire Rooster.

This matters! People born in early January often get their zodiac sign wrong. Always check the exact lunar dates to know for sure what is your Chinese zodiac sign.

2017: A Year That Shaped the World

Five moments that defined the Year of the Fire Rooster.

January 28, 2017
Year of the Fire Rooster Begins
The lunar new year ushered in the Fire Rooster, with red lanterns and dragon dances lighting up Chinatowns from London to Sydney. The Rooster is associated with confidence, decisiveness, and bold action—themes that proved fitting for the year.
January 20, 2017
Trump Inaugurated as President
Donald Trump was sworn in as the 45th U.S. president, eight days before the Fire Rooster year began. His inauguration drew millions of protesters to the Women's March the next day—the largest single-day protest in U.S. history.
August 12, 2017
Charlottesville Rally and Violence
A white nationalist rally in Charlottesville, Virginia, turned deadly when a car drove into counter-protesters, killing one and injuring dozens. The event intensified national debate over race, monuments, and the far right in America.
October 1, 2017
Las Vegas Shooting
A gunman opened fire on a country music festival from a hotel window in Las Vegas, killing 60 people and wounding more than 400. It was the deadliest mass shooting in modern U.S. history.
October 5, 2017
#MeToo Goes Global
The New York Times and New Yorker published investigations into sexual assault allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein. The reporting helped launch the global #MeToo movement, leading to the downfall of powerful figures across industries.
