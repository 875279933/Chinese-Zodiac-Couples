Chinese New Year 1937 landed on February 11, 1937, opening the Year of the Fire Ox on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Ding (丁) year, the Ding stem paired with the Chou (丑) branch.

The Ox holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Fire Ox year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1937

The 1937 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Fire Ox year.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1937, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1937, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1937, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1937, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1937, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1937, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

Core Meaning

The 1937 festival brought the Fire Ox year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Fire Ox year that began on February 11, 1937 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Ox.

The Ox Character

The Ox stands for steady effort. Patient, dependable, and willing to carry the load. An Ox year rewards families that put in the work and keep their promises.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1937, these small signs were everywhere.

🔥

The Fire Element

Fire years spark. They bring warmth, energy, and the nerve to start something that was only talk before.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 1937

The 1937 festival opened the Fire Ox year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1936-1945

Temple Fairs

The temple fair was the social event of the season. Stalls sold sugar figures, dried fruit, and paper toys, while opera stages drew crowds from early morning. In 1937, the fair was where a child spent their first red-envelope money.

Market Life

In the week before the new year, the market ran late into the evening. Shoppers bargained over cloth, fish, and fireworks while lanterns swung overhead. The lanes stayed loud until the last family had its supplies home.

Homemade Decorations

Store-bought decorations were a luxury most skipped. Families cut their own paper window flowers, brushed their own couplets, and pasted them up with rice paste. The work took an afternoon and gave the house its only fresh coat of the year.

Family Letters

For those working far from home, a letter was the closest thing to a seat at the table. Families read the old letters aloud and wrote new ones, so that even absent relatives had a place in the festival.

A Story from 1937

The meaning of the 1937 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Fire Ox year.

Xiao Mei, letter carrier, a busy district in Beijing.

Meet Xiao Mei, a letter carrier living in a busy district in Beijing. 1937 was the year Xiao Mei almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Xiao Mei dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Xiao Mei reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Xiao Mei sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Xiao Mei had tasted all year. The Fire Ox year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1937 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1937 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1937?

The festival fell on February 11, 1937. It marked the start of the Year of the Fire Ox on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1937 in the Chinese zodiac?

1937 is the Year of the Ox, specifically the Fire Ox. The Fire Ox year ran from February 11, 1937 to February 1, 1938 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1937?

In 1937, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1937?

1937 is a Fire year. In the five-element cycle, Fire years bring their own character to the Ox sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 11, 1937.
