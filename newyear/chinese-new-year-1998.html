Spring Festival Customs in 1998

The 1998 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Earth Tiger year.

  • 🧹

    Spring Cleaning

    A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1998, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

  • 🎇

    Firecrackers

    At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1998, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

  • 🏯

    Temple Visits

    On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1998, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

  • 🔥

    Ancestor Offerings

    Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1998, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

  • 🍲

    Reunion Dinner

    The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 1998, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

  • 🎁

    Red Envelopes

    Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 1998, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

The Travel Rush

Chunyun, the new-year travel rush, filled every train, bus, and ferry for weeks. In 1998, workers crossed provinces to reach home, and the reunion felt earned in a new way.

🛍

Goods on Every Shelf

Factory-made decorations, packaged gift boxes, and imported fruit filled the stores. The careful shopping of earlier decades gave way to abundance, and the question shifted from whether you could find it to which brand you wanted.

📺

The Gala and the Skits

The television gala's comedy skits set the jokes for the whole year. Families quoted them at each other through the holiday visits, and a good skit could define the season.

📱

Phone Greetings

Long-distance calls home became affordable, and on the first morning the phone rang steadily. Relatives who could not make the trip sent their wishes over the line, sometimes all at once.

A Story from 1998

The meaning of the 1998 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Earth Tiger year.

A postcard from 1998

Meet Rong Hua, a potter living in a farming village in Sichuan. 1998 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it became one Rong Hua would not forget. It started with a small disaster: the kitchen stove broke on New Year's Eve morning, right when the reunion meal was meant to be cooked. The house went quiet. Then neighbors stepped in. One lent a portable stove, another sent a pot of steaming rice, and a shopkeeper kept her door open an extra hour so the family could buy a replacement. By late afternoon the kitchen worked again, and by evening the table was full. The meal that year tasted better, maybe because it almost did not happen. Rong Hua took a simple lesson from the Earth Tiger year: good fortune often looks like neighbors looking out for each other. After midnight, with firecrackers rolling down the street, Rong Hua wrote a line in a notebook: remember the stove.

1998 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1998 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1998?

The festival fell on January 28, 1998. It marked the start of the Year of the Earth Tiger on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1998 in the Chinese zodiac?

1998 is the Year of the Tiger, specifically the Earth Tiger. The Earth Tiger year ran from January 28, 1998 to February 15, 1999 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1998?

In 1998, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1998?

1998 is a Earth year. In the five-element cycle, Earth years bring their own character to the Tiger sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 28, 1998.
