Spring Festival Customs in 2025

In 2025, as always, Spring Festival was shaped by the rituals people chose. From food to decoration to ceremony, each custom connected households to a shared heritage. Here are six of the most important customs observed during the Wood Snake year.

Ancestor Worship

Some families visited graves before the holiday, cleaning the stones and leaving offerings. Others held the ceremony at home, around a table with photographs and incense. Either way, the intention was the same: to say thank you. In 2025, the quiet act of remembrance felt more important than any public celebration.

🎁

Spring Couplets and Door Gods

Upstairs windows got smaller red decorations, too -- the inverted character for fortune, paper cuttings of the zodiac animal, and simple lucky phrases. The effect was a house that glowed red from the outside. In 2025, preparing all this paper took a full afternoon.

🍭

New Year Sweets and Snacks

Every household kept a tray of sweets and snacks ready for visitors. Candied winter melon, roasted melon seeds, sesame cookies, and sweet rice cakes were common. Each item carried a wish -- melon seeds for fertility, candied lotus root for harmony, sesame for long life. In 2025, these trays were refilled constantly.

🎓

Reunion Dinner

Long tables, short chairs, and steam rising above it all -- that was New Year's Eve. Every dish carried a wish: the fish represented having more than enough, the dumplings resembled ancient money, and the round tangyuan symbolized the family coming together. In 2025, the menu was planned weeks in advance.

🧵

Red Envelopes

Children waited most eagerly for the hongbao. Grandparents slipped them quietly into waiting palms, always with a soft blessing and a reminder to study hard. The red paper itself was considered lucky; the money inside was secondary. In 2025, a few coins could make a child's whole holiday.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

A holiday visit was not just a chat. It included tea, snacks, the exchange of auspicious phrases, and often a meal. Hosts took pride in having something to offer every guest, even unexpected ones. In 2025, households kept boxes of candies and tins of tea ready at all times during the first week of the year.
