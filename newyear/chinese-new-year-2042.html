Preparation started early. Houses were cleaned from top to bottom, debts were settled where possible, and new items were bought so that nothing worn or broken carried into the new year. Shopping in 2042 was a long, slow pleasure -- a chance to stroll, to bargain gently, and to plan every detail of the coming festival.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

A holiday visit was not just a chat. It included tea, snacks, the exchange of auspicious phrases, and often a meal. Hosts took pride in having something to offer every guest, even unexpected ones. In 2042, households kept boxes of candies and tins of tea ready at all times during the first week of the year.

Reunion Dinner

The reunion dinner was where the year's stories were told. Grandparents recalled festivals from their youth, parents quietly counted the blessings of the past twelve months, and children asked for seconds of everything. In 2042, this meal was the emotional center of the holiday.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

For children, nothing matched the thrill of watching a lion dance. The big-eyed costume, the bobbing head, the loud music -- it all felt like magic. Boys tried to imitate the movements in the courtyard afterward. In 2042, a visiting lion troupe could be the highlight of a child's entire holiday.

🎇

Firecrackers and Fireworks

Setting off firecrackers at midnight was (and still is) one of the most thrilling parts of Chinese New Year. The loud bangs were meant to scare away the mythical beast Nian and any lingering bad luck from the old year. In smaller towns and villages in 2042, the sound echoed through the night sky for hours.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

Some families set aside a special item each year -- a silk scarf for the daughter, a woolen vest for the son, a sturdy pair of shoes for the father. These items were worn on festival days and stored carefully for the rest of the year. In 2042, such pieces were sometimes the only new clothing a family bought.
