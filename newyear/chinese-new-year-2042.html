Spring Festival Customs in 2042

The 2042 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Water Dog year.

  • 🎈

    Lantern Night

    On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 2042, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

  • 🎁

    Spring Couplets

    Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 2042, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

  • 🪐

    Lion and Dragon Dances

    A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 2042, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

  • 👪

    Visiting Relatives

    The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 2042, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

  • 🧹

    Spring Cleaning

    A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 2042, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

  • 🎇

    Firecrackers

    At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 2042, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

A postcard from 2042

Meet Jian Guo, a farmer living in a mining settlement in Shanxi. 2042 was the year Jian Guo almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Jian Guo dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Jian Guo reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Jian Guo sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Jian Guo had tasted all year.
