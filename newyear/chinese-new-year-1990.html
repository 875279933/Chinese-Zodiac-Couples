Spring Festival Customs in 1990

The 1990 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Metal Horse year.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1990, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1990, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

🔥

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1990, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1990, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

🎁

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 1990, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

👗

New Clothes

Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 1990, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

The 1990 festival brought the Metal Horse year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Metal Horse year that began on January 27, 1990 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Horse.

The Horse Character

The Horse loves movement and company. Energetic, open, and quick to travel. A Horse year pulls people out of the house and into the street.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1990, these small signs were everywhere.

⚔

The Metal Element

Metal years sharpen. They cut away what is wasted and leave what is solid and useful.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Snapshot: 1986-1995
🍲

Fuller Tables

The reunion dinner in 1990 carried dishes that had been absent for years. Whole fish, a range of braised meats, and store-bought sweets found their way back, and the table could barely hold them all.

👗

New Clothes, New Styles

Ready-made clothes in bright colors replaced the tailored and mended outfits of earlier years. Children in 1990 picked their festival wear from racks, not from a tailor's order.

🎇

Fireworks at Midnight

Firecrackers came back in force, and the midnight sky over a city in 1990 was bright and loud for a solid hour. The smoke was thick, but no one minded.

📺

The Gala as Ritual

The television gala settled into a fixed part of the evening. Families timed the dumplings to the midnight countdown, and the two became inseparable.

A Story from 1990

The meaning of the 1990 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Metal Horse year.

A postcard from 1990

Meet Fen Fang, a teacher living in a port alley in Quanzhou. 1990 was the year Fen Fang finally understood what Chinese New Year was for. Until then, Fen Fang had cared mostly about new clothes, red envelopes, and a few days off. But that winter, the elder relative who usually hosted the reunion dinner fell ill. Without a host, the family briefly wondered whether the dinner would happen at all. Then, quietly, people stepped up. An aunt offered her kitchen. An uncle took over the shopping. A cousin, normally shy, found an old calligraphy set and wrote the spring couplets. By New Year's Eve the family was gathered around a different table in a different room, but everything important was the same. The dishes carried their old meanings, the altar was set with care, and the room stayed warm. For Fen Fang, the Metal Horse year was a lesson: tradition lives in people, not in places.

1990 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1990 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1990?

The festival fell on January 27, 1990. It marked the start of the Year of the Metal Horse on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1990 in the Chinese zodiac?

1990 is the Year of the Horse, specifically the Metal Horse. The Metal Horse year ran from January 27, 1990 to February 14, 1991 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1990?

In 1990, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1990?

1990 is a Metal year. In the five-element cycle, Metal years bring their own character to the Horse sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 27, 1990.
