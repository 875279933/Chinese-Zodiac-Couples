Spring Festival Customs in 1943

The 1943 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Water Goat year.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1943, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1943, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1943, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1943, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1943, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

🔥

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1943, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

Core Meaning

The 1943 festival brought the Water Goat year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Water Goat year that began on February 5, 1943 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Goat.

🐐

The Goat Character

The Goat stands for gentleness and craft. Creative, kind, and attentive to beauty. A Goat year asks families to tend to the small things that make a home feel like one.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1943, these small signs were everywhere.

💧

The Water Element

Water years flow. They favor adaptability, quiet connections, and finding a way around obstacles.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 1943

The 1943 festival opened the Water Goat year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1936-1945
🎈

Temple Fairs

The temple fair was the social event of the season. Stalls sold sugar figures, dried fruit, and paper toys, while opera stages drew crowds from early morning. In 1943, the fair was where a child spent their first red-envelope money.

🛍

Market Life

In the week before the new year, the market ran late into the evening. Shoppers bargained over cloth, fish, and fireworks while lanterns swung overhead. The lanes stayed loud until the last family had its supplies home.

✂

Homemade Decorations

Store-bought decorations were a luxury most skipped. Families cut their own paper window flowers, brushed their own couplets, and pasted them up with rice paste. The work took an afternoon and gave the house its only fresh coat of the year.

✉

Family Letters

For those working far from home, a letter was the closest thing to a seat at the table. Families read the old letters aloud and wrote new ones, so that even absent relatives had a place in the festival.

A Story from 1943

The meaning of the 1943 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Water Goat year.

Meet Qiang Li, a tailor living in a hillside hamlet in Yunnan. 1943 was the year Qiang Li almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Qiang Li dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Qiang Li reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Qiang Li sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Qiang Li had tasted all year. The Water Goat year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1943 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1943 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1943?

What animal is 1943 in the Chinese zodiac?

1943 is the Year of the Goat, specifically the Water Goat. The Water Goat year ran from February 5, 1943 to January 25, 1944 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1943?

In 1943, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1943?

1943 is a Water year. In the five-element cycle, Water years bring their own character to the Goat sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 5, 1943.
