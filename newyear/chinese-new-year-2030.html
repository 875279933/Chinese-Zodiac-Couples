Spring Festival Customs in 2030

In 2030, as always, Spring Festival was shaped by the rituals people chose. From food to decoration to ceremony, each custom connected households to a shared heritage. Here are six of the most important customs observed during the Metal Dog year.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

On the first few days of the new year, people visited relatives and friends, exchanging greetings and good wishes. Married couples visited the wife's family on the second day, a tradition that brought households together in a warm, orderly flow. In 2030, social calls were carefully planned days in advance.

🏯

Temple Visits and Prayers

On the first day of the new year, many families visited the local temple to light incense and offer prayers. The temples were busiest at dawn, when visitors hoped to be the first to make their offerings -- a practice believed to bring exceptional good fortune. In 2030, temple courtyards filled up quickly with families in their new clothes.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

Some households went further than cleaning. Walls were whitewashed, broken windowpanes were replaced, and the door itself got a fresh coat of red paint if one had been saved up. The goal was to make the house feel new. In 2030, these small home improvements were a kind of festival preparation in their own right.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

New clothing was more than vanity; it was symbolic. Starting the year in fresh garments represented starting the year with a clean appearance and a fresh spirit. For families of limited means, a single new item -- a scarf, a pair of socks, a small hat -- was enough to feel the tradition had been honored. In 2030, many children received just that.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

The lion dancers arrived at dawn, their cymbals and drums announcing the procession from blocks away. Shopkeepers stood in doorways with small offerings, hoping the lion would stop at their entrance and bow -- a traditional gesture for bringing prosperity to the business. In 2030, the best troupes drew crowds that stretched down entire streets.

🧵

Red Envelopes

Hongbao -- red envelopes containing money -- were given to children and unmarried young adults. The red color was believed to bring good luck and ward off evil spirits. In 2030, the amounts were modest, but the gesture carried deep meaning and warmth.

The sibling was clumsy at first -- the ink ran, the characters were too large, the paper got wrinkled -- but by the third evening, the work started to look respectable. The characters were a little wobbly, the ink was a little uneven, but the words were honest and meant something. Neighbors who walked past the door sometimes glanced twice at the writing, then smiled; they could tell it was a child's work, and they approved. Guang would later say that this small act -- letting a child write something important -- was what the Metal Dog year meant for the family. The festival, after all, was not about perfection. It was about passing something on.
