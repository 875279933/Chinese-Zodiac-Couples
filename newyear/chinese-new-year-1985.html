Chinese New Year 1985 landed on January 20, 1985, opening the Year of the Wood Ox on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Yi (乙) year, the Yi stem paired with the Chou (丑) branch. The Ox holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Wood Ox year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1985

The 1985 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Ox year.

  • 🎇

    Firecrackers

    At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1985, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

  • 🏯

    Temple Visits

    On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1985, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

  • 🔥

    Ancestor Offerings

    Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1985, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

  • 🧹

    Spring Cleaning

    A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1985, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

  • 🎁

    Red Envelopes

    Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 1985, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

  • 👗

    New Clothes

    Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 1985, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

Core Meaning

The 1985 festival brought the Wood Ox year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Wood Ox year that began on January 20, 1985 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Ox.

🐮

The Ox Character

The Ox stands for steady effort. Patient, dependable, and willing to carry the load. An Ox year rewards families that put in the work and keep their promises.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1985, these small signs were everywhere.

🌲

The Wood Element

Wood years grow. They favor patience, learning, and steady expansion, the way a tree adds rings each season.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 1985

The 1985 festival opened the Wood Ox year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1977-1985
📺

The Television Gala

From 1983 on, the Spring Festival Gala on television gave families a new reason to stay up past midnight. The set ran for hours, and even those who talked through it kept it on, glancing up for the skits and songs.

🛍

Returning Markets

Markets filled again with fireworks, dried fruit, and bolts of red cloth. Shoppers who had made do for years suddenly had choices, and the lanes in 1985 were loud with bargaining.

🪒

New Appliances

A family that had saved for a television or a sewing machine often brought it home just before the festival, so that it could be shown off to visiting relatives. The appliance became part of the celebration.

🚇

Travel Home

As work sent people farther afield, the trip home for the new year grew longer and more crowded. The train station in 1985 was a sea of bundles and sleeping children, all heading the same direction.

A Story from 1985

The meaning of the 1985 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Ox year.

Meet Xiao Mei, a letter carrier living in a busy district in Beijing. 1985 was the year Xiao Mei almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Xiao Mei dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Xiao Mei reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Xiao Mei sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Xiao Mei had tasted all year. The Wood Ox year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1985 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1985 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1985?

The festival fell on January 20, 1985. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Ox on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1985 in the Chinese zodiac?

1985 is the Year of the Ox, specifically the Wood Ox. The Wood Ox year ran from January 20, 1985 to February 8, 1986 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1985?

In 1985, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1985?

1985 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Ox sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 20, 1985.
