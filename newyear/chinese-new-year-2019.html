Festival Positioning

2019 marked the move into the Year of the Earth Pig, the 12th position in the 12-year zodiac cycle.

Chinese New Year 2019 landed on February 5, 2019, opening the Year of the Earth Pig on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Ji (己) year, the Ji stem paired with the Hai (亥) branch.

The Pig holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Earth Pig year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 2019

The 2019 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Earth Pig year.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 2019, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 2019, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 2019, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 2019, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 2019, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

🔥

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 2019, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

Core Meaning

The 2019 festival brought the Earth Pig year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Earth Pig year that began on February 5, 2019 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Pig.

🐖

The Pig Character

The Pig stands for plenty and good humor. Generous, easygoing, and fond of a full table. A Pig year asks families to enjoy what they have earned.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2019, these small signs were everywhere.

🏔

The Earth Element

Earth years steady. They suit saving, building, and laying foundations that will hold for years.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 2019

The 2019 festival opened the Earth Pig year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 2016-2025
💰

Mobile Red Envelopes

Red envelopes went to the phone in earnest. Groups of relatives sent money back and forth in a running game through the holiday, and children learned to grab them with a thumb.

📱

Video Reunions

When a family member could not get home, a video call propped on the table brought them into the dinner. The connection dropped now and then, but the face on the screen still got a toast.

🚴

Food Delivery Feasts

In cities, families ordered part of the reunion dinner from restaurants and apps. The kitchen still did the centerpiece dishes, but the table filled out with delivery boxes.

🚆

Travel, Faster

High-speed rail cut the trip home from days to hours. In 2019, the station was still crowded, but the crowds moved through faster, and more people made it home for the eve.

A Story from 2019

The meaning of the 2019 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Earth Pig year.

Meet Hui Ying, a carpenter living in a fishing island in Fujian. 2019 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it became one Hui Ying would not forget. It started with a small disaster: the kitchen stove broke on New Year's Eve morning, right when the reunion meal was meant to be cooked. The house went quiet. Then neighbors stepped in. One lent a portable stove, another sent a pot of steaming rice, and a shopkeeper kept her door open an extra hour so the family could buy a replacement. By late afternoon the kitchen worked again, and by evening the table was full. The meal that year tasted better, maybe because it almost did not happen. Hui Ying took a simple lesson from the Earth Pig year: good fortune often looks like neighbors looking out for each other. After midnight, with firecrackers rolling down the street, Hui Ying wrote a line in a notebook: remember the stove.

2019 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 2019 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 2019?

The festival fell on February 5, 2019. It marked the start of the Year of the Earth Pig on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 2019 in the Chinese zodiac?

2019 is the Year of the Pig, specifically the Earth Pig. The Earth Pig year ran from February 5, 2019 to January 24, 2020 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 2019?

In 2019, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 2019?

2019 is a Earth year. In the five-element cycle, Earth years bring their own character to the Pig sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 5, 2019.
