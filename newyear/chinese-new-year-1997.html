Festival Positioning

1997 marked the move into the Year of the Fire Ox, the 2nd position in the 12-year zodiac cycle.

Chinese New Year 1997 landed on February 7, 1997, opening the Year of the Fire Ox on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Ding (丁) year, the Ding stem paired with the Chou (丑) branch.

The Ox holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Fire Ox year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1997

The 1997 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Fire Ox year.

  • 🪐

    Lion and Dragon Dances

    A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1997, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

  • 👪

    Visiting Relatives

    The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1997, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

  • 🎈

    Lantern Night

    On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1997, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

  • 🎁

    Spring Couplets

    Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1997, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

  • 🏯

    Temple Visits

    On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1997, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

  • 🔥

    Ancestor Offerings

    Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1997, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

Cultural Context of 1997

The 1997 festival opened the Fire Ox year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1996-2005
🚇

The Travel Rush

Chunyun, the new-year travel rush, filled every train, bus, and ferry for weeks. In 1997, workers crossed provinces to reach home, and the reunion felt earned in a new way.

🛍

Goods on Every Shelf

Factory-made decorations, packaged gift boxes, and imported fruit filled the stores. The careful shopping of earlier decades gave way to abundance, and the question shifted from whether you could find it to which brand you wanted.

📺

The Gala and the Skits

The television gala's comedy skits set the jokes for the whole year. Families quoted them at each other through the holiday visits, and a good skit could define the season.

📱

Phone Greetings

Long-distance calls home became affordable, and on the first morning the phone rang steadily. Relatives who could not make the trip sent their wishes over the line, sometimes all at once.

A Story from 1997

The meaning of the 1997 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Fire Ox year.

A postcard from 1997

Meet Xiao Mei, a letter carrier living in a busy district in Beijing. 1997 was the year Xiao Mei almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Xiao Mei dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Xiao Mei reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Xiao Mei sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Xiao Mei had tasted all year. The Fire Ox year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1997 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1997 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1997?

The festival fell on February 7, 1997. It marked the start of the Year of the Fire Ox on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1997 in the Chinese zodiac?

1997 is the Year of the Ox, specifically the Fire Ox. The Fire Ox year ran from February 7, 1997 to January 27, 1998 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1997?

In 1997, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1997?

1997 is a Fire year. In the five-element cycle, Fire years bring their own character to the Ox sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 7, 1997.
