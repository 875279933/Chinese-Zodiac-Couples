Festival Positioning

1948 marked the move into the Year of the Earth Rat, the 1st position in the 12-year zodiac cycle.

Chinese New Year 1948 landed on February 10, 1948, opening the Year of the Earth Rat on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Wu (戊) year, the Wu stem paired with the Zi (子) branch.

The Rat holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Earth Rat year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1948

The 1948 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Earth Rat year.

01

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1948, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

02

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1948, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

03

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1948, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

04

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1948, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

05

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1948, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

06

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1948, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

Cultural Context of 1948

The 1948 festival opened the Earth Rat year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1946-1955
👪

Returning Relatives

After years apart, relatives found their way back to the family home. Some arrived thin, some arrived with new families, and the table grew to fit them all. The reunion dinner in 1948 was, for many, the first in a long time that felt complete.

🍲

Neighborhood Feasts

When one household could not manage a full dinner alone, neighbors pooled stoves and ingredients. A shared meal down the lane replaced the private one in the kitchen, and the cooking smell belonged to everyone.

👗

Mending and Making

New clothes were scarce, so old ones were taken in, let out, and dyed red where they could be. A child's festival outfit in 1948 was often three garments stitched into one.

🏯

Quiet Temple Visits

Temples opened their gates, and families came with incense and small offerings. The visits were shorter and quieter than before, but they restored a rhythm people had missed.

A Story from 1948

The meaning of the 1948 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Earth Rat year.

Meet Bo Chen, a shop clerk living in a tea-growing village in Anhui. 1948 was the year Bo Chen finally understood what Chinese New Year was for. Until then, Bo Chen had cared mostly about new clothes, red envelopes, and a few days off. But that winter, the elder relative who usually hosted the reunion dinner fell ill. Without a host, the family briefly wondered whether the dinner would happen at all. Then, quietly, people stepped up. An aunt offered her kitchen. An uncle took over the shopping. A cousin, normally shy, found an old calligraphy set and wrote the spring couplets. By New Year's Eve the family was gathered around a different table in a different room, but everything important was the same. The dishes carried their old meanings, the altar was set with care, and the room stayed warm. For Bo Chen, the Earth Rat year was a lesson: tradition lives in people, not in places.

1948 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1948 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1948?

The festival fell on February 10, 1948. It marked the start of the Year of the Earth Rat on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1948 in the Chinese zodiac?

1948 is the Year of the Rat, specifically the Earth Rat. The Earth Rat year ran from February 10, 1948 to January 28, 1949 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1948?

In 1948, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1948?

1948 is a Earth year. In the five-element cycle, Earth years bring their own character to the Rat sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 10, 1948.
