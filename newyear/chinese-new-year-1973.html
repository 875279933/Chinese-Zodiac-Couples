Chinese New Year 1973 landed on February 3, 1973, opening the Year of the Water Ox on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Gui (癸) year, the Gui stem paired with the Chou (丑) branch.

The Ox holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Water Ox year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1973

The 1973 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Water Ox year.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1973, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1973, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1973, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1973, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1973, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1973, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

Core Meaning

The 1973 festival brought the Water Ox year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Water Ox year that began on February 3, 1973 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Ox.

🐮

The Ox Character

The Ox stands for steady effort. Patient, dependable, and willing to carry the load. An Ox year rewards families that put in the work and keep their promises.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1973, these small signs were everywhere.

💧

The Water Element

Water years flow. They favor adaptability, quiet connections, and finding a way around obstacles.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 1973

The 1973 festival opened the Water Ox year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1966-1976
✂

Paper Cuttings

Families kept the decorations going with scissors and red paper. Window flowers of the zodiac animal, the character for fortune turned upside down, and simple lucky phrases went up on every pane.

🏯

Quiet Temple Visits

Temple visits thinned out, but a few families still came at dawn to light a stick of incense. They stayed only a few minutes, yet those few minutes kept an old habit alive for the next generation.

🍲

Homemade Dumplings

The reunion meal leaned on what a household could make itself. Dumplings, rolled and folded around the table, became the center of the dinner, and the folding was the part children remembered.

✉

Letters Home

For those sent far away, a letter written before the eve was the closest thing to being there. Families read them aloud at the table and saved them in a box for the next year.

A Story from 1973

The meaning of the 1973 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Water Ox year.

A postcard from 1973

Meet Xiao Mei, a letter carrier living in a busy district in Beijing. 1973 was the year Xiao Mei almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Xiao Mei dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Xiao Mei reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Xiao Mei sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Xiao Mei had tasted all year. The Water Ox year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1973 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1973 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1973?

The festival fell on February 3, 1973. It marked the start of the Year of the Water Ox on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1973 in the Chinese zodiac?

1973 is the Year of the Ox, specifically the Water Ox. The Water Ox year ran from February 3, 1973 to January 22, 1974 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1973?

In 1973, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1973?

1973 is a Water year. In the five-element cycle, Water years bring their own character to the Ox sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 3, 1973.
