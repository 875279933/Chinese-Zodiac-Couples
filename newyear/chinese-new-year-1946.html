Spring Festival Customs in 1946

The 1946 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Fire Dog year.

01

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1946, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

02

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1946, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

03

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1946, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

04

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1946, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

05

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 1946, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

06

New Clothes

Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 1946, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

Cultural Context of 1946

The 1946 festival opened the Fire Dog year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1946-1955
👪

Returning Relatives

After years apart, relatives found their way back to the family home. Some arrived thin, some arrived with new families, and the table grew to fit them all. The reunion dinner in 1946 was, for many, the first in a long time that felt complete.

🍲

Neighborhood Feasts

When one household could not manage a full dinner alone, neighbors pooled stoves and ingredients. A shared meal down the lane replaced the private one in the kitchen, and the cooking smell belonged to everyone.

👗

Mending and Making

New clothes were scarce, so old ones were taken in, let out, and dyed red where they could be. A child's festival outfit in 1946 was often three garments stitched into one.

🏯

Quiet Temple Visits

Temples opened their gates, and families came with incense and small offerings. The visits were shorter and quieter than before, but they restored a rhythm people had missed.

A Story from 1946

The meaning of the 1946 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Fire Dog year.

Meet Jian Guo, a farmer living in a mining settlement in Shanxi. 1946 was the year Jian Guo almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Jian Guo dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Jian Guo reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Jian Guo sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Jian Guo had tasted all year. The Fire Dog year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1946 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1946 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1946?

The festival fell on February 2, 1946. It marked the start of the Year of the Fire Dog on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1946 in the Chinese zodiac?

1946 is the Year of the Dog, specifically the Fire Dog. The Fire Dog year ran from February 2, 1946 to January 21, 1947 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1946?

In 1946, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1946?

1946 is a Fire year. In the five-element cycle, Fire years bring their own character to the Dog sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 2, 1946.
