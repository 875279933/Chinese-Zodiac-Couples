Spring Festival Customs in 2038

In 2038, as always, Spring Festival was shaped by the rituals people chose. From food to decoration to ceremony, each custom connected households to a shared heritage. Here are six of the most important customs observed during the Earth Horse year.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

Spring cleaning was not just about dirt. Windows were washed, old newspapers were cleared, worn-out clothes were mended or replaced, and kitchens were scrubbed. The idea was to start the new year with an uncluttered home. In 2038, the cleaning was done a day or two before the festival so that the brooms could be put away before New Year's Day -- sweeping on the holiday itself was considered bad luck.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

Red was the preferred color for festival wear, but any cheerful color was welcome. Adults tended toward darker, more dignified outfits; children wore whatever made them happy. In 2038, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the characteristic image of the holiday.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

On the first few days of the new year, people visited relatives and friends, exchanging greetings and good wishes. Married couples visited the wife's family on the second day, a tradition that brought households together in a warm, orderly flow. In 2038, social calls were carefully planned days in advance.

🎇

Firecrackers and Fireworks

Some families preferred long, winding strings of firecrackers hung from rooftops; others lit individual fireworks that sent colored sparks spiraling up into the dark sky. Both styles were equally beloved. In 2038, firecracker shops stayed open late, their shelves piled high with red paper tubes.

🎁

Spring Couplets and Door Gods

Upstairs windows got smaller red decorations, too -- the inverted character for fortune, paper cuttings of the zodiac animal, and simple lucky phrases. The effect was a house that glowed red from the outside. In 2038, preparing all this paper took a full afternoon.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A proper dragon dance needed dozens of men, each holding a pole that supported a section of the long, colorful body. As they moved in unison, the dragon seemed to swim through the street, coiling and uncoiling. In 2038, such performances were reserved for the biggest village fairs and city festivals.
