Spring Festival Customs in 2020

In 2020, as always, Spring Festival was shaped by the rituals people chose. From food to decoration to ceremony, each custom connected households to a shared heritage. Here are six of the most important customs observed during the Metal Rat year.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

New clothing was more than vanity; it was symbolic. Starting the year in fresh garments represented starting the year with a clean appearance and a fresh spirit. For families of limited means, a single new item -- a scarf, a pair of socks, a small hat -- was enough to feel the tradition had been honored. In 2020, many children received just that.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

The first words spoken on New Year's Day mattered. Traditional greetings were exchanged -- wishes for health, prosperity, safe children, and successful work. Younger generations knelt or bowed to their elders; elders gave blessings and often a small red envelope. In 2020, these formal gestures were still followed with real care.

🙏

Ancestor Worship

Before the dinner, the family gathered quietly before the ancestor altar. Bowing three times, lighting incense, placing small cups of tea and rice wine -- these were gestures of remembrance. In 2020, even families that rarely discussed the past still found time for this quiet ceremony.

🏯

Temple Visits and Prayers

Temple visits were not only about asking for things. They were also about thankfulness -- for a safe year past, for the health of family members, for enough to eat. Families lit three sticks of incense each, bowed before the main altar, and sometimes left small offerings of fruit or oil. In 2020, the larger temples held special ceremonies throughout the first week.

🎁

Spring Couplets and Door Gods

The doorway was the first thing to receive attention. New spring couplets went up -- one vertical strip on each side of the door, one shorter one across the top -- their black ink glowing against the bright red paper. Door god images were taped to the door itself, standing guard. In 2020, careful households replaced every couplet before midnight.

👜

New Year Shopping and Preparation

Preparation started early. Houses were cleaned from top to bottom, debts were settled where possible, and new items were bought so that nothing worn or broken carried into the new year. Shopping in 2020 was a long, slow pleasure -- a chance to stroll, to bargain gently, and to plan every detail of the coming festival.

Good Fortune

Prosperity is not only about money. In the Chinese tradition, a prosperous year is one where the family stays healthy, relationships stay warm, and there is enough food on the table. The Rat year in 2020 was celebrated with these hopes in mind.

Family and Heritage

The festival renews family ties. Children see their grandparents, siblings travel long distances to sit at the same table, and relatives who have been out of touch exchange warm greetings. In 2020, this renewal was the deepest purpose of the celebration.

Historical Context of 2020

The world in 2020 was a place of tension and transformation. Here's what was happening when the Metal Rat year began.

🌎

China in 2020

China in 2020 was navigating the challenges of the COVID-19 pandemic and its aftermath. Travel restrictions, lockdowns, and health concerns reshaped how people celebrated Chinese New Year. Many families celebrated apart for the first time, connecting through screens instead of sharing a table.

🌐

A World Changed

The early 2020s were defined by the global pandemic. For Chinese New Year in 2020, the traditional travel rush was disrupted, temple fairs were canceled, and gatherings were limited. Yet the spirit of the festival endured -- people found creative ways to celebrate, from virtual reunions to balcony fireworks.

🏛

Daily Life and Resilience

Families in 2020 faced a new kind of challenge during Chinese New Year. But the festival's core message -- hope, renewal, and the bond of family -- resonated more than ever. People understood that being apart was temporary, and the tradition would outlast the crisis.

📚

Tradition as Stability

In the face of a global health crisis, Chinese New Year provided emotional anchor. The rituals, even when adapted for remote celebration, gave people a sense of continuity and comfort. It was a reminder that tradition is not about the form it takes, but the meaning it carries.

A Story from 2020

The true meaning of Chinese New Year is often found not in grand history books, but in small human memories. Here is one such story from the Metal Rat year.

Meet Tian Yu, a schoolteacher in a farming village in Sichuan. In 2020, Tian was preparing for Chinese New Year the way he always did -- by helping at home, running errands for the family, and quietly hoping the coming Metal Rat year would bring something good. The week before the festival, the market was busier than usual. Tian came home with bags of rice, a string of firecrackers wrapped in brown paper, and a small red envelope the shopkeeper slipped to him with a wink. On New Year's Eve, the house was full. The stove stayed hot from morning until late at night, the siblings ran errands between the kitchen and the dining table, and the grandmother sat by the window folding paper envelopes while telling stories of festivals from her youth. At midnight, Tian lit the longest string of firecrackers the family could afford, and the whole neighborhood watched the sparks climb into the dark sky. Years later, Tian would remember 2020 not for anything dramatic, but for the feeling of the family gathered in one room -- steam from the hot dishes curling against the ceiling, red decorations glowing on every wall, and the quiet certainty that whatever came next, they would face it together.
