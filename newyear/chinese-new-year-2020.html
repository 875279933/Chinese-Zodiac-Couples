Spring Festival Customs in 2020

The 2020 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Metal Rat year.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 2020, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 2020, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 2020, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

🔥

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 2020, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

🍲

Reunion Dinner

The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 2020, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

🎁

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 2020, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

The Rat Character

The Rat opens the cycle. Quick, careful with resources, and good at finding a way through tight spots. A Rat year asks households to think ahead and make the most of what they have.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2020, these small signs were everywhere.

⚔

The Metal Element

Metal years sharpen. They cut away what is wasted and leave what is solid and useful.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 2020

The 2020 festival opened the Metal Rat year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 2016-2025
💰

Mobile Red Envelopes

Red envelopes went to the phone in earnest. Groups of relatives sent money back and forth in a running game through the holiday, and children learned to grab them with a thumb.

📱

Video Reunions

When a family member could not get home, a video call propped on the table brought them into the dinner. The connection dropped now and then, but the face on the screen still got a toast.

🚴

Food Delivery Feasts

In cities, families ordered part of the reunion dinner from restaurants and apps. The kitchen still did the centerpiece dishes, but the table filled out with delivery boxes.

🚆

Travel, Faster

High-speed rail cut the trip home from days to hours. In 2020, the station was still crowded, but the crowds moved through faster, and more people made it home for the eve.

A Story from 2020

The meaning of the 2020 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Metal Rat year.

Meet Bo Chen, a shop clerk living in a tea-growing village in Anhui. 2020 was the year Bo Chen finally understood what Chinese New Year was for. Until then, Bo Chen had cared mostly about new clothes, red envelopes, and a few days off. But that winter, the elder relative who usually hosted the reunion dinner fell ill. Without a host, the family briefly wondered whether the dinner would happen at all. Then, quietly, people stepped up. An aunt offered her kitchen. An uncle took over the shopping. A cousin, normally shy, found an old calligraphy set and wrote the spring couplets. By New Year's Eve the family was gathered around a different table in a different room, but everything important was the same. The dishes carried their old meanings, the altar was set with care, and the room stayed warm. For Bo Chen, the Metal Rat year was a lesson: tradition lives in people, not in places.

2020 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 2020 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 2020?

The festival fell on January 25, 2020. It marked the start of the Year of the Metal Rat on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 2020 in the Chinese zodiac?

2020 is the Year of the Rat, specifically the Metal Rat. The Metal Rat year ran from January 25, 2020 to February 11, 2021 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 2020?

In 2020, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 2020?

2020 is a Metal year. In the five-element cycle, Metal years bring their own character to the Rat sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 25, 2020.
