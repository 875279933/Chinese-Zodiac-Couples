Chinese New Year 2014 landed on January 31, 2014, opening the Year of the Wood Horse on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Jia (甲) year, the Jia stem paired with the Wu (午) branch.

Spring Festival Customs in 2014

The 2014 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Horse year.

01

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 2014, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

02

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 2014, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

03

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 2014, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

04

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 2014, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

05

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 2014, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

06

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 2014, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

The Horse Character

The Horse loves movement and company. Energetic, open, and quick to travel. A Horse year pulls people out of the house and into the street.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2014, these small signs were everywhere.

🌲

The Wood Element

Wood years grow. They favor patience, learning, and steady expansion, the way a tree adds rings each season.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 2014

The 2014 festival opened the Wood Horse year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 2006-2015

Text Message Greetings

On New Year's Day, phones lit up with text greetings, sometimes arriving in batches. A household in 2014 might send the same message to a hundred contacts before lunch.

Online Shopping

Gift boxes, decorations, and even fresh ingredients came from online shops, delivered to the door in the days before the festival. The market trip shrank to a single afternoon.

Digital Red Envelopes

The first digital red envelopes appeared, sent phone to phone with a few taps. They did not replace the paper ones, but they reached relatives who lived too far to hand a bill to.

The Gala in HD

The television gala went high-definition, and the cameras grew more elaborate each year. Families still talked through most of it, but the midnight countdown stayed the one moment everyone watched.

A Story from 2014

The meaning of the 2014 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Horse year.

Meet Fen Fang, a teacher living in a port alley in Quanzhou. 2014 was the year Fen Fang finally understood what Chinese New Year was for. Until then, Fen Fang had cared mostly about new clothes, red envelopes, and a few days off. But that winter, the elder relative who usually hosted the reunion dinner fell ill. Without a host, the family briefly wondered whether the dinner would happen at all. Then, quietly, people stepped up. An aunt offered her kitchen. An uncle took over the shopping. A cousin, normally shy, found an old calligraphy set and wrote the spring couplets. By New Year's Eve the family was gathered around a different table in a different room, but everything important was the same. The dishes carried their old meanings, the altar was set with care, and the room stayed warm. For Fen Fang, the Wood Horse year was a lesson: tradition lives in people, not in places.

2014 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 2014 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 2014?

The festival fell on January 31, 2014. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Horse on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 2014 in the Chinese zodiac?

2014 is the Year of the Horse, specifically the Wood Horse. The Wood Horse year ran from January 31, 2014 to February 18, 2015 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 2014?

In 2014, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 2014?

2014 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Horse sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 31, 2014.
