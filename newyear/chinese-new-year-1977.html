Spring Festival Customs in 1977

How did people actually celebrate Chinese New Year in 1977? The customs were deeply rooted in centuries of tradition, with regional variations that made each celebration unique. This year, families focused on six favorite rituals -- each one chosen for its special meaning and its place in the household's routine.

🍭

New Year Sweets and Snacks

Making the holiday snacks was a task shared by the women of the household. Rolling sesame cookies, pressing rice cakes, stirring the sugar syrup that coated candied fruit -- these were the smells and sounds of preparation. In 1977, a jar of the family's favorite sweets sat on every kitchen table.

🙏

Ancestor Worship

Paying respects to ancestors was a solemn and essential part of Chinese New Year. Families set up altars with food offerings, incense, and paper money to honor those who had passed. This ritual connected the living with their heritage and reinforced the importance of family continuity in 1977.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

The first words spoken on New Year's Day mattered. Traditional greetings were exchanged -- wishes for health, prosperity, safe children, and successful work. Younger generations knelt or bowed to their elders; elders gave blessings and often a small red envelope. In 1977, these formal gestures were still followed with real care.

🎓

Reunion Dinner

Nothing mattered more on New Year's Eve than sitting down together as a family. The table filled up slowly -- a whole steamed fish at one end, dumplings folded that morning at the other, a golden nian gao waiting for dessert. In 1977, mothers spent all day in the kitchen, and children were told to eat every last bite.

🎁

Spring Couplets and Door Gods

The doorway was the first thing to receive attention. New spring couplets went up -- one vertical strip on each side of the door, one shorter one across the top -- their black ink glowing against the bright red paper. Door god images were taped to the door itself, standing guard. In 1977, careful households replaced every couplet before midnight.

🎇

Firecrackers and Fireworks

Firecrackers had a practical as well as a symbolic purpose. Their noise was meant to drive off anything unwelcome, leaving the house clean and auspicious for the coming year. In 1977, families set them off in doorways and courtyards, lighting one string after another until the ground glowed red with paper scraps.

Family and Heritage

The festival renews family ties. Children see their grandparents, siblings travel long distances to sit at the same table, and relatives who have been out of touch exchange warm greetings. In 1977, this renewal was the deepest purpose of the celebration.

🌿

Prosperity and Abundance

In Chinese culture, the Snake represents prosperity earned through intelligence and careful judgment. Celebrating the Fire Snake's arrival was an invitation to welcome abundance into the home for the year ahead.

🔥

Fire Element Energy

Fire brings the qualities of its element to the Snake year. Fire years bring warmth, energy, passion, and a willingness to venture into something new.

🔴

A Fresh Start

Each new lunar year offers a chance to begin again. In 1977, under the sign of the Snake, families saw the festival as an invitation to reset their habits, their homes, and their hopes.

Historical Context of 1977

The world in 1977 was a place of tension and transformation. Here's what was happening when the Fire Snake year began.

🌎

China in 1977

China in 1977 was emerging from the Cultural Revolution and entering a new era under Deng Xiaoping's reform policies. The country was opening up, and traditional customs like Chinese New Year were being revived after years of suppression. For the first time in a decade, people could celebrate openly and with genuine enthusiasm.

🌐

Reform and Opening Up

The late 1970s marked a turning point for China. Deng Xiaoping's reforms began to transform the economy and society. Traditional festivals, including Chinese New Year, were no longer discouraged. Instead, they were embraced as part of China's cultural heritage. The Spring Festival of 1977 was a celebration not just of the new year, but of freedom.

🏛

Daily Life and Resilience

After years of restraint, families in 1977 celebrated Chinese New Year with renewed energy. Markets filled with holiday goods, temples reopened, and lion dances returned to the streets. The festival became a symbol of the new era -- vibrant, hopeful, and unapologetically Chinese.

📚

Tradition as Stability

The revival of Chinese New Year in the late 1970s showed how deeply embedded the festival is in Chinese life. It wasn't just a holiday -- it was an expression of identity. After the Cultural Revolution, reclaiming these traditions was a way for people to reconnect with their heritage.

A Story from 1977

The true meaning of Chinese New Year is often found not in grand history books, but in small human memories. Here is one such story from the Fire Snake year.

Meet Yu Chen, a writer in a market town in Henan. In 1977, Yu was preparing for Chinese New Year the way he always did -- by helping at home, running errands for the family, and quietly hoping the coming Fire Snake year would bring something good. The week before the festival, the market was busier than usual. Yu came home with bags of rice, a string of firecrackers wrapped in brown paper, and a small red envelope the shopkeeper slipped to him with a wink. On New Year's Eve, the house was full. The stove stayed hot from morning until late at night, the siblings ran errands between the kitchen and the dining table, and the grandmother sat by the window folding paper envelopes while telling stories of festivals from her youth. At midnight, Yu lit the longest string of firecrackers the family could afford, and the whole neighborhood watched the sparks climb into the dark sky. Years later, Yu would remember 1977 not for anything dramatic, but for the feeling of the family gathered in one room -- steam from the hot dishes curling against the ceiling, red decorations glowing on every wall, and the quiet certainty that whatever came next, they would face it together.
