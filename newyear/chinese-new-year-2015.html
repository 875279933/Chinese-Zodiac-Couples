Spring Festival Customs in 2015

The 2015 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Goat year.

01

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 2015, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

02

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 2015, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

03

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 2015, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

04

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 2015, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

05

Reunion Dinner

The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 2015, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

06

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 2015, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

The 2015 festival brought the Wood Goat year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Wood Goat year that began on February 19, 2015 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Goat.

The Goat Character

The Goat stands for gentleness and craft. Creative, kind, and attentive to beauty. A Goat year asks families to tend to the small things that make a home feel like one.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2015, these small signs were everywhere.

🌲

The Wood Element

Wood years grow. They favor patience, learning, and steady expansion, the way a tree adds rings each season.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 2015

The 2015 festival opened the Wood Goat year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 2006-2015

Text Message Greetings

On New Year's Day, phones lit up with text greetings, sometimes arriving in batches. A household in 2015 might send the same message to a hundred contacts before lunch.

Online Shopping

Gift boxes, decorations, and even fresh ingredients came from online shops, delivered to the door in the days before the festival. The market trip shrank to a single afternoon.

Digital Red Envelopes

The first digital red envelopes appeared, sent phone to phone with a few taps. They did not replace the paper ones, but they reached relatives who lived too far to hand a bill to.

The Gala in HD

The television gala went high-definition, and the cameras grew more elaborate each year. Families still talked through most of it, but the midnight countdown stayed the one moment everyone watched.

A Story from 2015

The meaning of the 2015 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Goat year.

Meet Qiang Li, a tailor living in a hillside hamlet in Yunnan. 2015 was the year Qiang Li almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Qiang Li dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Qiang Li reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Qiang Li sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Qiang Li had tasted all year. The Wood Goat year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

2015 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 2015 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 2015?

The festival fell on February 19, 2015. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Goat on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 2015 in the Chinese zodiac?

2015 is the Year of the Goat, specifically the Wood Goat. The Wood Goat year ran from February 19, 2015 to February 7, 2016 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 2015?

In 2015, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 2015?

2015 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Goat sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 19, 2015.
