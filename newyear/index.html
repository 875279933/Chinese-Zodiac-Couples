Chinese New Year

The most important festival in Chinese culture, celebrated for over 3,000 years. Discover its history, traditions, and exact dates from 1936 to 2042.

What Is Chinese New Year?

The Spring Festival marks the start of a new year on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

The Spring Festival is the most widely celebrated holiday in Chinese culture. Its roots reach back more than three thousand years to the Shang Dynasty, growing out of ancient agricultural rites and seasonal observances. Today the holiday brings together families across China and across the global Chinese diaspora.

The Chinese calendar is lunisolar, not purely solar. That means the holiday falls on a different Gregorian date each year, always between January 21 and February 20. The date is fixed by the second new moon after the winter solstice.

Each year pairs one of twelve zodiac animals — Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig — with one of five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water). Together they form a 60-year cycle that has organized Chinese timekeeping for centuries. You can see how each sign shapes character in our zodiac personality guide.

The celebration runs 15 days, from New Year's Eve through the Lantern Festival. Families travel home, share meals, honor ancestors, and reset their households for the year ahead.

Traditions and Customs

The festival carries a set of customs that families return to each year. Each one ties the household to a shared habit.

🎓

Family Reunion Dinner

The New Year's Eve dinner is the most important meal of the year. Families gather from near and far to share dishes that carry meaning — fish for abundance, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for a long life.

🧵

Red Envelopes

Known as hongbao, red envelopes with cash are given to children and unmarried adults. The red color is tied to the holiday and has been part of the tradition for generations.

🎇

Fireworks and Firecrackers

Setting off fireworks is one of the most recognizable Spring Festival traditions. The noise and light mark the turn of the year. The custom links back to the old story of Nian, a beast said to appear at year's end.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

These performances are a centerpiece of public celebration. The dragon dance calls for a team of dozens, each holding a pole that supports the long body. It draws the biggest crowds of any festival event.

🎁

Red Decorations

Homes and streets fill with red lanterns, spring couplets, and paper cuttings. Red is the dominant color of the holiday, tied to the season's themes of renewal and warmth.

🕑

15-Day Celebration

The festival runs from New Year's Eve to the Lantern Festival. Each day carries its own customs, from visiting relatives on the first days to lighting lanterns on the fifteenth.

Quick Answers

The basics on the festival, in short.

What is Chinese New Year?

It is the start of the new year on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar, also called the Spring Festival or Lunar New Year. Families mark it with reunion dinners, decorations, fireworks, and visits to relatives.

When does it fall?

The date moves each year on the Gregorian calendar, landing between January 21 and February 20. The date is set by the second new moon after the winter solstice.

How long does Chinese New Year last?

The traditional celebration runs 15 days, from New Year's Eve to the Lantern Festival. The first three days carry the most activity — family gatherings, temple visits, and exchanges of red envelopes.

What are the main traditions?

The family reunion dinner on New Year's Eve, red envelopes (hongbao) for children and unmarried adults, fireworks, red lanterns and couplets, lion and dragon dances, and visits to temples and relatives.
