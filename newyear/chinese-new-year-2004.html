Chinese New Year 2004 landed on January 22, 2004, opening the Year of the Wood Monkey on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Jia (甲) year, the Jia stem paired with the Shen (申) branch. The Monkey holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Wood Monkey year carries its own tone. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes families carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 2004

The 2004 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Monkey year.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 2004, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

🔥

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 2004, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 2004, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 2004, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

👗

New Clothes

Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 2004, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

🛍

New Year Shopping

In the days before the festival, the market ran late. Stalls of red paper, dried fruit, fireworks, and cloth crowded the lanes. In 2004, shoppers carried bundles home through streets strung with lanterns.

Core Meaning

The 2004 festival brought the Wood Monkey year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Wood Monkey year that began on January 22, 2004 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Monkey.

🐒

The Monkey Character

The Monkey is clever and restless. Quick-witted, playful, and good with a workaround. A Monkey year rewards flexibility and a sense of humor.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2004, these small signs were everywhere.

🌲

The Wood Element

Wood years grow. They favor patience, learning, and steady expansion, the way a tree adds rings each season.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 2004

The 2004 festival opened the Wood Monkey year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1996-2005
🚇

The Travel Rush

Chunyun, the new-year travel rush, filled every train, bus, and ferry for weeks. In 2004, workers crossed provinces to reach home, and the reunion felt earned in a new way.

🛍

Goods on Every Shelf

Factory-made decorations, packaged gift boxes, and imported fruit filled the stores. The careful shopping of earlier decades gave way to abundance, and the question shifted from whether you could find it to which brand you wanted.

📺

The Gala and the Skits

The television gala's comedy skits set the jokes for the whole year. Families quoted them at each other through the holiday visits, and a good skit could define the season.

📱

Phone Greetings

Long-distance calls home became affordable, and on the first morning the phone rang steadily. Relatives who could not make the trip sent their wishes over the line, sometimes all at once.

A Story from 2004

The meaning of the 2004 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Monkey year.

Lin Wei, rail porter, a market street in Xi'an.

Meet Lin Wei, a rail porter living in a market street in Xi'an. 2004 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it became one Lin Wei would not forget. It started with a small disaster: the kitchen stove broke on New Year's Eve morning, right when the reunion meal was meant to be cooked. The house went quiet. Then neighbors stepped in. One lent a portable stove, another sent a pot of steaming rice, and a shopkeeper kept her door open an extra hour so the family could buy a replacement. By late afternoon the kitchen worked again, and by evening the table was full. The meal that year tasted better, maybe because it almost did not happen. Lin Wei took a simple lesson from the Wood Monkey year: good fortune often looks like neighbors looking out for each other. After midnight, with firecrackers rolling down the street, Lin Wei wrote a line in a notebook: remember the stove.

2004 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 2004 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 2004?

The festival fell on January 22, 2004. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Monkey on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 2004 in the Chinese zodiac?

2004 is the Year of the Monkey, specifically the Wood Monkey. The Wood Monkey year ran from January 22, 2004 to February 8, 2005 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 2004?

In 2004, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 2004?

2004 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Monkey sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 22, 2004.
