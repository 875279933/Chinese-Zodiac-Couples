Chinese New Year 1945 landed on February 13, 1945, opening the Year of the Wood Rooster on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Yi (乙) year, the Yi stem paired with the You (酉) branch. The Rooster holds a set place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Wood Rooster year carries its own tone. Spring Festival Customs in 1945

The 1945 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Rooster year.

👗

New Clothes

Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 1945, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

🛍

New Year Shopping

In the days before the festival, the market ran late. Stalls of red paper, dried fruit, fireworks, and cloth crowded the lanes. In 1945, shoppers carried bundles home through streets strung with lanterns.

🍲

Reunion Dinner

The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 1945, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

🎁

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 1945, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1945, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1945, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

Core Meaning

The 1945 festival brought the Wood Rooster year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Wood Rooster year that began on February 13, 1945 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Rooster.

🐓

The Rooster Character

The Rooster is direct and orderly. Honest, punctual, and proud of a clean record. A Rooster year asks households to get their affairs in order.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1945, these small signs were everywhere.

🌲

The Wood Element

Wood years grow. They favor patience, learning, and steady expansion, the way a tree adds rings each season.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

Cultural Context of 1945

The 1945 festival opened the Wood Rooster year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1936-1945
🎈

Temple Fairs

The temple fair was the social event of the season. Stalls sold sugar figures, dried fruit, and paper toys, while opera stages drew crowds from early morning. In 1945, the fair was where a child spent their first red-envelope money.

🛍

Market Life

In the week before the new year, the market ran late into the evening. Shoppers bargained over cloth, fish, and fireworks while lanterns swung overhead. The lanes stayed loud until the last family had its supplies home.

✂

Homemade Decorations

Store-bought decorations were a luxury most skipped. Families cut their own paper window flowers, brushed their own couplets, and pasted them up with rice paste. The work took an afternoon and gave the house its only fresh coat of the year.

✉

Family Letters

For those working far from home, a letter was the closest thing to a seat at the table. Families read the old letters aloud and wrote new ones, so that even absent relatives had a place in the festival.

A Story from 1945

The meaning of the 1945 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Rooster year.

Meet Mei Lan, a noodle-stall owner living in a canal town in Hangzhou. 1945 was the year Mei Lan finally understood what Chinese New Year was for. Until then, Mei Lan had cared mostly about new clothes, red envelopes, and a few days off. But that winter, the elder relative who usually hosted the reunion dinner fell ill. Without a host, the family briefly wondered whether the dinner would happen at all. Then, quietly, people stepped up. An aunt offered her kitchen. An uncle took over the shopping. A cousin, normally shy, found an old calligraphy set and wrote the spring couplets. By New Year's Eve the family was gathered around a different table in a different room, but everything important was the same. The dishes carried their old meanings, the altar was set with care, and the room stayed warm. For Mei Lan, the Wood Rooster year was a lesson: tradition lives in people, not in places.

1945 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1945 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1945?

The festival fell on February 13, 1945. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Rooster on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1945 in the Chinese zodiac?

1945 is the Year of the Rooster, specifically the Wood Rooster. The Wood Rooster year ran from February 13, 1945 to February 1, 1946 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1945?

In 1945, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1945?

1945 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Rooster sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 13, 1945.
