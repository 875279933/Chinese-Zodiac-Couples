Spring Festival Customs in 1979

The 1979 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Earth Goat year.

01

Ancestor Offerings

Before the meal, families set places for those who came before. Incense, a cup of wine, a bow. In 1979, the ancestor altar was the quiet center of the loudest week of the year.

02

Spring Cleaning

A few days before the new year, every household swept, scrubbed, and mended. Windows came clean, old papers went out, and worn clothes got patched. The point was to step into the new year unburdened. In 1979, brooms went back in the corner before New Year's Day, since sweeping on the holiday itself was said to sweep the luck out.

03

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1979, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

04

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1979, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

05

New Year Shopping

In the days before the festival, the market ran late. Stalls of red paper, dried fruit, fireworks, and cloth crowded the lanes. In 1979, shoppers carried bundles home through streets strung with lanterns.

06

Reunion Dinner

The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 1979, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

Core Meaning

The 1979 festival brought the Earth Goat year, with its own blend of meaning. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar carries its own character, and the Earth Goat year that began on January 28, 1979 set the tone for the months ahead. For families, it carried a set of steady wishes: health, a full table, safety, and a home in good order. Read more on the Year of the Goat.

🐐

The Goat Character

The Goat stands for gentleness and craft. Creative, kind, and attentive to beauty. A Goat year asks families to tend to the small things that make a home feel like one.

🌿

Symbols on the Table

Abundance in Chinese tradition shows up through symbols. A fish on the table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 1979, these small signs were everywhere.

🏔

The Earth Element

Earth years steady. They suit saving, building, and laying foundations that will hold for years.

👪

Home and Heritage

At its center, Chinese New Year is about coming together. The festival gives families a chance to honor the people who came before, mend the ties that frayed, and reset the household before the year turns.

The Television Gala

From 1983 on, the Spring Festival Gala on television gave families a new reason to stay up past midnight. The set ran for hours, and even those who talked through it kept it on, glancing up for the skits and songs.

🛍

Returning Markets

Markets filled again with fireworks, dried fruit, and bolts of red cloth. Shoppers who had made do for years suddenly had choices, and the lanes in 1979 were loud with bargaining.

🪒

New Appliances

A family that had saved for a television or a sewing machine often brought it home just before the festival, so that it could be shown off to visiting relatives. The appliance became part of the celebration.

🚇

Travel Home

As work sent people farther afield, the trip home for the new year grew longer and more crowded. The train station in 1979 was a sea of bundles and sleeping children, all heading the same direction.

A Story from 1979

The meaning of the 1979 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Earth Goat year.

Meet Qiang Li, a tailor living in a hillside hamlet in Yunnan. 1979 was the year Qiang Li almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Qiang Li dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Qiang Li reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Qiang Li sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Qiang Li had tasted all year. The Earth Goat year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.

1979 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1979 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1979?

The festival fell on January 28, 1979. It marked the start of the Year of the Earth Goat on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1979 in the Chinese zodiac?

How was the festival celebrated in 1979?

In 1979, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1979?

1979 is a Earth year. In the five-element cycle, Earth years bring their own character to the Goat sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 28, 1979.
