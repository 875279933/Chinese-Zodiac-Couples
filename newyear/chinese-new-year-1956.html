1956 marked the move into the Year of the Fire Monkey, the 9th position in the 12-year zodiac cycle.

Chinese New Year 1956 landed on February 12, 1956, opening the Year of the Fire Monkey on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Bing (丙) year, the Bing stem paired with the Shen (申) branch.

Spring Festival Customs in 1956

The 1956 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Fire Monkey year.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1956, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1956, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1956, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1956, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1956, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1956, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

Cultural Context of 1956

The 1956 festival opened the Fire Monkey year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1956-1965
🍲

Shared Stoves

In many neighborhoods, families cooked on shared stoves set up in the lane. The reunion meal came together dish by dish as each household contributed something, and the smell of braised meat drifted through the whole street.

📟

Shift Holidays

Factories and work units arranged time off in shifts so that some families ate on the eve and others on the first day. The exact hour mattered less than the fact that the meal happened at all.

🎈

Handmade Lanterns

Store-bought lanterns were hard to find, so fathers and grandfathers built them from bamboo and paper. A child in 1956 carried a lantern shaped like a rabbit that had taken three evenings to make.

📺

Radio Evenings

With a radio in the room, the family could hear opera and storytelling while they folded dumplings. The set was small and the signal came and went, but it gave the evening a soundtrack.

A Story from 1956

The meaning of the 1956 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Fire Monkey year.

Lin Wei, rail porter, a market street in Xi'an.

Meet Lin Wei, a rail porter living in a market street in Xi'an. 1956 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it became one Lin Wei would not forget. It started with a small disaster: the kitchen stove broke on New Year's Eve morning, right when the reunion meal was meant to be cooked. The house went quiet. Then neighbors stepped in. One lent a portable stove, another sent a pot of steaming rice, and a shopkeeper kept her door open an extra hour so the family could buy a replacement. By late afternoon the kitchen worked again, and by evening the table was full. The meal that year tasted better, maybe because it almost did not happen. Lin Wei took a simple lesson from the Fire Monkey year: good fortune often looks like neighbors looking out for each other. After midnight, with firecrackers rolling down the street, Lin Wei wrote a line in a notebook: remember the stove.

1956 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1956 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1956?

The festival fell on February 12, 1956. It marked the start of the Year of the Fire Monkey on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1956 in the Chinese zodiac?

1956 is the Year of the Monkey, specifically the Fire Monkey. The Fire Monkey year ran from February 12, 1956 to January 30, 1957 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1956?

In 1956, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1956?

1956 is a Fire year. In the five-element cycle, Fire years bring their own character to the Monkey sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on February 12, 1956.
