Spring Festival Customs in 2023

Chinese New Year customs in 2023 mixed timeless traditions with small, personal touches. Different households emphasized different things, but six practices stood out as central to how the festival was lived that winter. Here is what a typical family's season might have included.

🎓

Reunion Dinner

The New Year's Eve dinner was the centerpiece of the celebration. Families gathered around tables laden with dishes chosen for their symbolic meaning -- fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, and sticky rice cake for a higher year ahead. In 2023, this meal was often the most elaborate of the entire year.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

A holiday visit was not just a chat. It included tea, snacks, the exchange of auspicious phrases, and often a meal. Hosts took pride in having something to offer every guest, even unexpected ones. In 2023, households kept boxes of candies and tins of tea ready at all times during the first week of the year.

🏯

Temple Visits and Prayers

Some temples had stone statues or sacred trees that visitors touched as they walked past, hoping to receive a little extra luck. Others had large bronze urns where incense sticks were collected, their smoke rising slowly toward the roof. In 2023, these quiet places of worship were full of people moving slowly and respectfully.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

Children were put to work, too, even if their cleaning was only half serious. Dusting, straightening shelves, carrying out small piles of rubbish -- everyone had a part. The reward was a home that felt fresh and bright for the holiday. In 2023, the day of cleaning ended with a simple but satisfying meal together.

👗

Wearing New Clothes

Some families set aside a special item each year -- a silk scarf for the daughter, a woolen vest for the son, a sturdy pair of shoes for the father. These items were worn on festival days and stored carefully for the rest of the year. In 2023, such pieces were sometimes the only new clothing a family bought.

🎇

Firecrackers and Fireworks

Firecrackers had a practical as well as a symbolic purpose. Their noise was meant to drive off anything unwelcome, leaving the house clean and auspicious for the coming year. In 2023, families set them off in doorways and courtyards, lighting one string after another until the ground glowed red with paper scraps.

The Year's Energy

Every combination of animal and element has its own character. Water years bring flexibility, communication skills, and a talent for adapting to change.

🌿

Traditional Blessings

Abundance in Chinese tradition is shown through symbolism. A fish on the dinner table stands for having more than enough. Dumplings stand for wealth. Tangerines stand for good fortune. In 2023, these small symbols were everywhere.

🌿

Good Fortune

Prosperity is not only about money. In the Chinese tradition, a prosperous year is one where the family stays healthy, relationships stay warm, and there is enough food on the table. The Rabbit year in 2023 was celebrated with these hopes in mind.

🌿

Prosperity and Abundance

In Chinese culture, the Rabbit represents quiet, comfortable prosperity -- a life well arranged and well enjoyed. Celebrating the Water Rabbit's arrival was an invitation to welcome abundance into the home for the year ahead.

Historical Context of 2023

The world in 2023 was a place of tension and transformation. Here's what was happening when the Water Rabbit year began.

🌎

China in 2023

China in 2023 was navigating the challenges of the COVID-19 pandemic and its aftermath. Travel restrictions, lockdowns, and health concerns reshaped how people celebrated Chinese New Year. Many families celebrated apart for the first time, connecting through screens instead of sharing a table.

🌐

A World Changed

The early 2020s were defined by the global pandemic. For Chinese New Year in 2023, the traditional travel rush was disrupted, temple fairs were canceled, and gatherings were limited. Yet the spirit of the festival endured -- people found creative ways to celebrate, from virtual reunions to balcony fireworks.

🏛

Daily Life and Resilience

Families in 2023 faced a new kind of challenge during Chinese New Year. But the festival's core message -- hope, renewal, and the bond of family -- resonated more than ever. People understood that being apart was temporary, and the tradition would outlast the crisis.

📚

Tradition as Stability

In the face of a global health crisis, Chinese New Year provided emotional anchor. The rituals, even when adapted for remote celebration, gave people a sense of continuity and comfort. It was a reminder that tradition is not about the form it takes, but the meaning it carries.

A Story from 2023

The true meaning of Chinese New Year is often found not in grand history books, but in small human memories. Here is one such story from the Water Rabbit year.

Meet Rong Hua, a nurse born in a riverside village in Zhejiang. 2023 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it turned into one of the holidays Rong would remember forever. It started with a small disaster: the stove in the family kitchen broke on New Year's Eve morning, right before the holiday meal was supposed to be cooked. The house felt suddenly, unexpectedly quiet. But neighbors rallied. One lent a portable stove; another sent over a large pot of already-steaming rice. A nearby shopkeeper kept her doors open a little longer just so the family could buy a replacement. By mid-afternoon, the kitchen was working again, if a little awkwardly, and by evening the dining table was full of dishes after all. The meal that year tasted better than usual, perhaps because it had nearly not happened. Rong learned something simple about the Water Rabbit year: that good fortune sometimes looks like ordinary people helping each other out. After midnight, as the firecrackers went off up and down the street, Rong wrote a short note in a notebook -- 'Remember the stove.'
