Festival Positioning

Chinese New Year 1970 marked the transition into the Year of the Metal Dog, a year shaped by the energy of both the Metal element and the Dog's distinctive character.

Chinese New Year 1970 landed on February 6, kicking off the Year of the Metal Dog on the traditional lunisolar calendar. In the 60-year cycle of Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches, this was the Geng (庚) year -- the Metal stem paired with the Dog branch.

The Dog holds a special place in the Chinese zodiac, and a Metal Dog year carries its own distinctive energy. Each zodiac year brings a different flavor to the Spring Festival, shaping the hopes and expectations people carry into the months ahead.

Spring Festival Customs in 1970

In 1970, as always, Spring Festival was shaped by the rituals people chose. From food to decoration to ceremony, each custom connected households to a shared heritage. Here are six of the most important customs observed during the Metal Dog year.

🧹

Spring Cleaning

Children were put to work, too, even if their cleaning was only half serious. Dusting, straightening shelves, carrying out small piles of rubbish -- everyone had a part. The reward was a home that felt fresh and bright for the holiday. In 1970, the day of cleaning ended with a simple but satisfying meal together.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

A holiday visit was not just a chat. It included tea, snacks, the exchange of auspicious phrases, and often a meal. Hosts took pride in having something to offer every guest, even unexpected ones. In 1970, households kept boxes of candies and tins of tea ready at all times during the first week of the year.

🎓

Reunion Dinner

The reunion dinner was where the year's stories were told. Grandparents recalled festivals from their youth, parents quietly counted the blessings of the past twelve months, and children asked for seconds of everything. In 1970, this meal was the emotional center of the holiday.

🎇

Firecrackers and Fireworks

Firecrackers had a practical as well as a symbolic purpose. Their noise was meant to drive off anything unwelcome, leaving the house clean and auspicious for the coming year. In 1970, families set them off in doorways and courtyards, lighting one string after another until the ground glowed red with paper scraps.

🙏

Ancestor Worship

Some families visited graves before the holiday, cleaning the stones and leaving offerings. Others held the ceremony at home, around a table with photographs and incense. Either way, the intention was the same: to say thank you. In 1970, the quiet act of remembrance felt more important than any public celebration.

🍭

New Year Sweets and Snacks

Every household kept a tray of sweets and snacks ready for visitors. Candied winter melon, roasted melon seeds, sesame cookies, and sweet rice cakes were common. Each item carried a wish -- melon seeds for fertility, candied lotus root for harmony, sesame for long life. In 1970, these trays were refilled constantly.

Core Meaning

The Metal Dog year carries a unique blend of symbolism -- renewal, vitality, and the promise of fresh starts. Chinese New Year in 1970, the Year of the Metal Dog, was celebrated with hopes that reflected the zodiac animal's traditional character and the element's particular energy. Each year in the Chinese lunisolar calendar has its own meaning. The Metal Dog year beginning in 1970 brought expectations shaped by centuries of tradition. For families celebrating in 1970, the Metal Dog year carried a specific set of wishes: for health, for prosperity, for safety, and for the quiet happiness of a home in order.

⚙

The Year's Energy

Every combination of animal and element has its own character. Metal years reward discipline, sharp thinking, structured effort, and careful planning.

🌿

Good Fortune

Prosperity is not only about money. In the Chinese tradition, a prosperous year is one where the family stays healthy, relationships stay warm, and there is enough food on the table. The Dog year in 1970 was celebrated with these hopes in mind.

🌿

Prosperity and Abundance

In Chinese culture, the Dog represents steady, reliable prosperity built on loyalty and consistent effort. Celebrating the Metal Dog's arrival was an invitation to welcome abundance into the home for the year ahead.

🔴

A Fresh Start

Each new lunar year offers a chance to begin again. In 1970, under the sign of the Dog, families saw the festival as an invitation to reset their habits, their homes, and their hopes.

Historical Context of 1970

The world in 1970 was a place of tension and transformation. Here's what was happening when the Metal Dog year began.

🌎

China in 1970

China in 1970 was in the midst of the Cultural Revolution. Traditional customs were actively suppressed, and many families celebrated Chinese New Year quietly or not at all. Red Guards discouraged temple fairs, lion dances, and ancestor worship. Yet the festival survived underground, kept alive by families who refused to let their traditions die.

🌐

The Cultural Revolution Era

The Cultural Revolution (1966-1976) was one of the most disruptive periods in Chinese history. Traditional culture was attacked, intellectuals were persecuted, and society was turned upside down. Chinese New Year was rebranded as a 'revolutionized' holiday, stripped of its religious and cultural elements. But beneath the surface, people still held onto the old ways.

🏛

Daily Life and Resilience

For families in 1970, celebrating Chinese New Year required discretion. A quiet meal at home, a whispered blessing for the ancestors, a red envelope passed under the table -- these small acts of cultural defiance kept the spirit of the festival alive during its darkest period.

📚

Tradition as Stability

The fact that Chinese New Year survived the Cultural Revolution is a testament to its deep roots in Chinese identity. No amount of political pressure could erase a tradition that had been practiced for thousands of years. When the Cultural Revolution ended, the festival emerged stronger than ever.

A Story from 1970

The true meaning of Chinese New Year is often found not in grand history books, but in small human memories. Here is one such story from the Metal Dog year.

Meet Fu Qiang, an assistant born in a quiet neighborhood in Shanghai. 1970 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it turned into one of the holidays Fu would remember forever. It started with a small disaster: the stove in the family kitchen broke on New Year's Eve morning, right before the holiday meal was supposed to be cooked. The house felt suddenly, unexpectedly quiet. But neighbors rallied. One lent a portable stove; another sent over a large pot of already-steaming rice. A nearby shopkeeper kept her doors open a little longer just so the family could buy a replacement. By mid-afternoon, the kitchen was working again, if a little awkwardly, and by evening the dining table was full of dishes after all. The meal that year tasted better than usual, perhaps because it had nearly not happened. Fu learned something simple about the Metal Dog year: that good fortune sometimes looks like ordinary people helping each other out. After midnight, as the firecrackers went off up and down the street, Fu wrote a short note in a notebook -- 'Remember the stove.'
