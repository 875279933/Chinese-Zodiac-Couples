A proper dragon dance needed dozens of men, each holding a pole that supported a section of the long, colorful body. As they moved in unison, the dragon seemed to swim through the street, coiling and uncoiling. In 2039, such performances were reserved for the biggest village fairs and city festivals.

Ancestor worship was not a grand spectacle; it was the small things done consistently. A bowl of rice on the altar, a few sticks of incense left to burn down, paper spirit money folded carefully and set aside for burning. In 2039, these daily acts were what kept the connection to past generations alive.

Reunion Dinner

The New Year's Eve dinner was the centerpiece of the celebration. Families gathered around tables laden with dishes chosen for their symbolic meaning -- fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, and sticky rice cake for a higher year ahead. In 2039, this meal was often the most elaborate of the entire year.

Spring Couplets and Door Gods

Couplets were chosen with real thought. A family running a shop would pick phrases about prosperity; a household with schoolchildren would choose verses about study and wisdom. Many families in 2039 wrote their own, carefully practicing the characters until they felt just right.

Temple Visits and Prayers

Making a wish at the temple was a serious but gentle act. Parents whispered quiet prayers for their children; grandparents prayed for the whole family's health. The phrases were simple and ancient. In 2039, the temple visit was still a regular part of the festival for millions of families.

👩

Visiting Relatives and Friends

The first words spoken on New Year's Day mattered. Traditional greetings were exchanged -- wishes for health, prosperity, safe children, and successful work. Younger generations knelt or bowed to their elders; elders gave blessings and often a small red envelope. In 2039, these formal gestures were still followed with real care.
