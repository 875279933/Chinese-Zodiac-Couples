Spring Festival Customs in 1995

The 1995 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Pig year.

🎁

Spring Couplets

Red paper strips went up on every door, black ink brushed in pairs, with a shorter line across the top. Door gods stood guard at the entrance. In 1995, a good calligrapher could fill half a street with orders in the days before the festival.

🪐

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 1995, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

👪

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 1995, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

🎈

Lantern Night

On the fifteenth night, lanterns went up. Children carried rabbits and fish made of paper and wire, candlelit from inside. In 1995, the festival closed the way it opened, with light.

🎇

Firecrackers

At midnight the sky lit up. Long strings of firecrackers unrolled from rooftops, and children clapped hands over their ears. The noise chased the old year off. In 1995, the smoke hung low over the rooftops and mixed with the smell of cooking oil.

🏯

Temple Visits

On the first morning, families walked to the temple with incense. The early arrivals hoped to be first to the altar. In 1995, temple courtyards filled with new clothes and low murmurs before sunrise.

Cultural Context of 1995

The 1995 festival opened the Wood Pig year against the backdrop of its time. Here is a look at the cultural and social life families carried into the festival.

Cultural Snapshot: 1986-1995

Fuller Tables

The reunion dinner in 1995 carried dishes that had been absent for years. Whole fish, a range of braised meats, and store-bought sweets found their way back, and the table could barely hold them all.

New Clothes, New Styles

Ready-made clothes in bright colors replaced the tailored and mended outfits of earlier years. Children in 1995 picked their festival wear from racks, not from a tailor's order.

Fireworks at Midnight

Firecrackers came back in force, and the midnight sky over a city in 1995 was bright and loud for a solid hour. The smoke was thick, but no one minded.

The Gala as Ritual

The television gala settled into a fixed part of the evening. Families timed the dumplings to the midnight countdown, and the two became inseparable.

A Story from 1995

The meaning of the 1995 festival often lives less in records than in small memories. Here is one from the Wood Pig year.

Hui Ying, carpenter, a fishing island in Fujian.

Meet Hui Ying, a carpenter living in a fishing island in Fujian. 1995 was supposed to be an ordinary Chinese New Year, but it became one Hui Ying would not forget. It started with a small disaster: the kitchen stove broke on New Year's Eve morning, right when the reunion meal was meant to be cooked. The house went quiet. Then neighbors stepped in. One lent a portable stove, another sent a pot of steaming rice, and a shopkeeper kept her door open an extra hour so the family could buy a replacement. By late afternoon the kitchen worked again, and by evening the table was full. The meal that year tasted better, maybe because it almost did not happen. Hui Ying took a simple lesson from the Wood Pig year: good fortune often looks like neighbors looking out for each other. After midnight, with firecrackers rolling down the street, Hui Ying wrote a line in a notebook: remember the stove.

1995 Festival - Quick Answers

The basics on the 1995 festival, in short.

When was Chinese New Year in 1995?

The festival fell on January 31, 1995. It marked the start of the Year of the Wood Pig on the traditional Chinese lunisolar calendar.

What animal is 1995 in the Chinese zodiac?

1995 is the Year of the Pig, specifically the Wood Pig. The Wood Pig year ran from January 31, 1995 to February 18, 1996 on the Gregorian calendar.

How was the festival celebrated in 1995?

In 1995, families marked the festival with reunion dinners, ancestor offerings, firecrackers, red envelopes for children, spring couplets on doorways, and lion and dragon dances. The customs followed centuries-old tradition, with regional differences across China.

What element is 1995?

1995 is a Wood year. In the five-element cycle, Wood years bring their own character to the Pig sign, shaping the tone of the year that began on January 31, 1995.
