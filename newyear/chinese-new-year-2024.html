Spring Festival Customs in 2024

The 2024 festival kept the customs families returned to every year. Each one tied the household to a shared habit, from food to decoration to ceremony. These are six of the ones that mattered most in the Wood Dragon year.

01

Red Envelopes

Married adults tucked crisp bills into red envelopes and pressed them into children's hands. The amount mattered less than the gesture. In 2024, a child might carry a stack of hongbao home and count them late into the night.

02

New Clothes

Red was the favorite, but any bright color would do. Children wore what made them happy; adults leaned toward darker, quieter tones. In 2024, a child in a new red coat walking to a relative's house was the picture of the holiday.

03

New Year Shopping

In the days before the festival, the market ran late. Stalls of red paper, dried fruit, fireworks, and cloth crowded the lanes. In 2024, shoppers carried bundles home through streets strung with lanterns.

04

Reunion Dinner

The night before the new year, families gathered around one table. Fish for surplus, dumplings for wealth, long noodles for long life, a chicken left whole to keep the family whole. In 2024, this was the meal people traveled all year to reach.

05

Lion and Dragon Dances

A lion dance troupe needed two strong dancers and a drummer. A dragon dance needed dozens, each holding a pole that held up the long body. In 2024, such performances drew the biggest crowds of the fair.

06

Visiting Relatives

The first days of the year were for rounds of visits. Married couples called on the wife's family on the second day. In 2024, the route was planned days ahead, and every stop came with tea, snacks, and good wishes.

A postcard from 2024

Meet Yan Ling, a bicycle repairman living in a coastal neighborhood in Guangzhou. 2024 was the year Yan Ling almost did not make it home. The train was late, the platform was crowded, and the journey that should have taken hours stretched into a full day. Yan Ling dozed standing up, woke to snack sellers pushing through the car, and watched the winter fields pass in the dark. When the train finally pulled in, the streets were already lit with red lanterns and the air smelled like firecrackers. Yan Ling reached the family door just as the reunion meal was being set out. There was no time to change, no time to rest. Yan Ling sat down still wearing the coat from the road, and the first bite of the meal was the best Yan Ling had tasted all year. The Wood Dragon year began with a simple reminder: the hardest part of the holiday is getting there, and the rest takes care of itself.
