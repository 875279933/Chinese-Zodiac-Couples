2033 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2033 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Gui Chou

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2033 is the year of Gui Chou. "Gui" stands for Water in the Heavenly Stems, and "Chou" maps to the Ox in the Earthly Branches. This combo only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2033 lunar year kicks off on Jan 31, 2033 and wraps up on Feb 18, 2034. If your birthday lands in that window, you're a Water Ox. Born earlier in Jan 2033 -- before the 31st -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Yin Water Energy

Gui Water is Yin Water -- reflective, patient, and inward. It gives people born under its influence a calm, steady nature. Water anchors the Ox's work ethic with quiet persistence.

Total Solar Eclipse

A total solar eclipse crosses the eastern Pacific, Alaska, and parts of the North Atlantic. The path of totality also grazes the Russian Far East —the first total eclipse of the 2030s visible from North America.

The astronomical summer begins in the Northern Hemisphere. Beyond the calendar event, 2033 is expected to bring another record-setting hot summer across much of the US, Europe, and East Asia.

The annual Perseids light up the night sky, with up to 100 meteors per hour at peak. Best viewing is in the pre-dawn hours in the Northern Hemisphere —a slow, easy night for a Water Ox to sit back and enjoy.

Hybrid Solar Eclipse

A rare hybrid solar eclipse —part annular, part total —sweeps across the Pacific. Only a narrow band sees totality, but partial phases are visible across the western Americas and the South Pacific.
