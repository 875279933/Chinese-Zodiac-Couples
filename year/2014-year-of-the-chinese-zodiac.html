2014 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 2014 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Jia Wu

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2014 was the year of Jia Wu. "Jia" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Wu" maps to the Horse in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 2014

The 2014 lunar year kicked off on Jan 31, 2014 and wrapped up on Feb 18, 2015. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Horse. But if you were born earlier in Jan 2014 -- before the 31 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why Yang Wood Matters

The Jia Wood in the 2014 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yang Wood -- Yang energy is active, outward, and assertive. It pushes things forward and gives people born under its influence a natural confidence and drive. Wood adds growth and cooperation. People born in this year tend to be more collaborative and open-minded than others of their sign, always looking for ways to expand and improve.

2014 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 2014 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

2014 on the Western Calendar

2014 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Wood Horse's growth-oriented, progressive energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 2014 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 2014, the Wood Horse period ran from Jan 31, 2014 through Feb 18, 2015.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 31 2014 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Horse. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters

If you're looking into family history or just trying to understand what the 2014 year of the Chinese zodiac really means, getting the calendar right is half the battle. The Chinese zodiac gives you a completely different way of reading personality and life path compared to Western astrology.

Big Events That Shaped 2014

2014 was the year of a viral ice bucket, a missing plane, and a Crimea crisis. Here are five events that stood out from the year.

March 2014

Russia Annexes Crimea

Vladimir Putin signed treaties to absorb Crimea 18 days after unmarked Russian troops took control of the peninsula. The move triggered the worst East-West tensions since the Cold War.

March 8, 2014

MH370 Disappears

Malaysia Airlines Flight 370 vanished with 239 people on board en route from Kuala Lumpur to Beijing. The search became the most expensive in aviation history at $150 million.

Summer 2014

Ice Bucket Challenge Goes Viral

The fundraiser hit Facebook feeds everywhere in July and August, raising $115 million for the ALS Association in 8 weeks. Pete Frates, who inspired the campaign, died in 2019.

March 2014

Ebola Outbreak

The West African epidemic killed 11,325 people across Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. The US deployed 2,800 troops to Liberia and 11 patients were treated on US soil.

Sept 18, 2014

Scottish Independence Vote

Scotland voted 55-45 to stay in the UK in a turnout of 84.6%, the highest for any election in the UK since 1959. Cameron promised more devolution hours after the result.
