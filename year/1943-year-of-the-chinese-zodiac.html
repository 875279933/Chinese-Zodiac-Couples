1943 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1943 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Gui Wei

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1943 was the year of Gui Wei. "Gui" stands for Yin Water in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Wei" maps to the Goat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1943

The 1943 lunar year kicked off on Feb 5, 1943 and wrapped up on Jan 24, 1944. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Water Goat. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1943 -- before the 5 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why Yin Water Matters

The Gui Water in the 1943 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yin Water -- Yin energy is quiet and inward, closer to dew than a river. Yin Water adds sensitivity, perceptiveness, and a way of reading people that the typical Goat year does not always have.
