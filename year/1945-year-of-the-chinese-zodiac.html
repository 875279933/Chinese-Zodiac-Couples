1945 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1945 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Yi You

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1945 was the year of Yi You. "Yi" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "You" maps to the Rooster in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1945

The 1945 lunar year kicked off on Feb 13, 1945 and wrapped up on Feb 1, 1946. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Rooster. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1945 -- before the 13 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why Yin Wood Matters

The Yi Wood in the 1945 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yin Wood -- Yin energy is receptive, inward, and intuitive. It gives people born under its influence a depth of feeling and a quiet strength that runs deeper than it appears. Wood adds growth and cooperation. People born in this year tend to be more collaborative and open-minded than others of their sign, always looking for ways to expand and improve.

1945 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1945 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1945 on the Western Calendar

1945 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Wood Rooster's growth-oriented, progressive energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1945 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Chinese New Year shifts each year, so the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1945, the Wood Rooster period ran from Feb 13, 1945 through Feb 1, 1946.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 12 1945 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Rooster. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Famous People Born in 1945

1945 is a year that just kept producing legends. Here are six Wood Roosters whose names are still in heavy rotation today.

Bob Marley

Born February 6 in Nine Mile, Jamaica. Reggae music's biggest ambassador, full stop. "Redemption Song," "No Woman No Cry," "One Love." Marley's music has been covered in more languages than most artists get played in.

Tom Selleck

Born January 29 in Detroit, Michigan. Magnum, P.I. alone would have earned him a place on this list. Throw in Friends, Blue Bloods, and a long line of westerns, and Selleck is one of those actors who somehow never goes out of style.

Steve Martin

Born August 14 in Waco, Texas. Stand-up, then The Jerk, then Roxanne, then Shopgirl, then Only Murders in the Building. Martin has spent fifty years moving between comedy, drama, writing, banjo playing, and the occasional art show -- and somehow stays sharp at all of them.

Goldie Hawn

Born November 21 in Washington, D.C. Cactus Flower, Private Benjamin, Overboard, The First Wives Club. Hawn won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress at 24 -- the youngest winner in that category at the time.

Eric Clapton

Born March 30 in Ripley, England. "Layla," "Tears in Heaven," "Wonderful Tonight." Three different inductions into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, which is the kind of thing that doesn't happen unless you basically changed guitar music three times.

Neil Young

Born November 12 in Toronto, Canada. "Heart of Gold," "Harvest Moon," "Rockin' in the Free World." Young has released forty-plus studio albums over fifty years, and the average quality hasn't dropped much. That kind of consistency is rare in any genre.
