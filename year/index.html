Year of the Chinese Zodiac

Every year in the Chinese calendar is ruled by one of twelve animals. Look up any year from 1936 to 2042 and find out which zodiac sign — and which element — defines it.

How the Year of the Chinese Zodiac Works

A quick primer on the system before you look up your year.

The Chinese zodiac follows a 12-year cycle, with each year tied to a specific animal: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. On top of that, every year also carries one of five elements — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, or Water — which adds another layer of meaning to the sign.

That means the full cycle (12 animals × 5 elements) takes 60 years to complete. So the Fire Rat year of 1936 won't come around again until 1996, and then not until 2056. This 60-year loop is why two people born in different Rat years can have noticeably different personalities — the element shifts the flavor of the animal.

One thing to keep in mind: the Chinese zodiac year starts on Chinese New Year, not January 1st. If your birthday falls in January or early February, double-check the exact date of Chinese New Year for your birth year. You might actually belong to the previous year's sign.
