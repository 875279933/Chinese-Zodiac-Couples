1973 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1973 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Gui Chou

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1973 was the year of Gui Chou. "Gui" stands for Water in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chou" maps to the Ox in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1973

The 1973 lunar year kicked off on Feb 3, 1973 and wrapped up on Jan 22, 1974. If your birthday falls in that window, you're an Water Ox. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1973 -- before the 3 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

People born in this year are more flexible and perceptive than others of their sign, with a natural feel for people and situations.

1973 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1973 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1973 on the Western Calendar

1973 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Water Ox's adaptable, flexible energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1973 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1973, the Water Ox period ran from Feb 3, 1973 through Jan 22, 1974.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 3 1973 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not an Ox. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters
