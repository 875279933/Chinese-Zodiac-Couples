The Year of Ding Hai

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1947 was the year of Ding Hai. "Ding" stands for Yin Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Hai" maps to the Pig in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1947

The 1947 lunar year kicked off on Jan 22, 1947 and wrapped up on Feb 9, 1948. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Pig. Born earlier in Jan 1947 -- before the 22 -- and you're the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1947 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1947 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1947 on the Western Calendar

1947 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Fire Pig's passionate, dynamic energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1947 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1947, the Fire Pig period ran from Jan 22, 1947 through Feb 9, 1948.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 22 1947 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Pig. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Famous People Born in 1947

1947 is a year stacked with rock stars, late-night hosts, and at least one governor. Here are six Fire Pigs whose names you have definitely heard.

Elton John

Born March 25 in Pinner, England. "Rocketman," "Tiny Dancer," "Your Song," "Crocodile Rock." Elton has sold more than 300 million records, an Oscar for The Lion King, and a Tony for The Lion King on Broadway. He retired from touring in 2023 -- but the farewell tour became the highest-grossing concert tour of all time.

David Bowie

Born January 8 in Brixton, England. "Space Oddity," "Heroes," "Let's Dance," "Under Pressure." Bowie spent fifty years zigzagging between genres -- glam, soul, electronica, krautrock -- and made every version of himself look inevitable.

Arnold Schwarzenegger

Born July 30 in Thal, Austria. Mr. Universe at 20. The Terminator, Predator, Total Recall. Then Governor of California from 2003 to 2011. Schwarzenegger turned a thick Austrian accent and a bodybuilding hobby into one of the most unusual career arcs in American public life.

David Letterman

Born April 12 in Indianapolis, Indiana. Late Night with David Letterman ran from 1982 to 1993. The Late Show ran from 1993 to 2015. In between, Dave built a reputation for on-air stunts that nobody else would air, and a desk-chewing habit that became its own running joke.

Carlos Santana

Born July 20 in Autlán, Mexico. "Smooth," "Black Magic Woman," "Oye Como Va." Ten Grammy Awards, three Latin Grammys, and a Rock and Roll Hall of Fame induction. Santana basically invented the sound of rock guitar with Latin jazz, and "Smooth" still gets a lot of radio play in 2025.

Brian May

Born July 19 in Hampton, London. Lead guitarist of Queen, and the voice behind the guitar solo in "Bohemian Rhapsody." May also holds a PhD in astrophysics, which is a combination of accomplishments that basically nobody else on this list can match.
