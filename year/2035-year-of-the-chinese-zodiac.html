2035 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2035 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Yi Mao

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2035 is the year of Yi Mao. "Yi" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems, and "Mao" maps to the Rabbit in the Earthly Branches. This combo only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2035 lunar year kicks off on Feb 7, 2035 and wraps up on Jan 27, 2036. If your birthday lands in that window, you're a Wood Rabbit. Born earlier in Feb 2035 -- before the 7th -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Yin Wood Energy

Yi Wood is Yin Wood -- flexible, gentle, and inward. It gives people born under its influence a patient, creative nature. Wood fuels the Rabbit's grace with quiet resilience.

Big Moments in 2035

From sky-shaking eclipses to global political moments, here's what 2035 has on the calendar. These are the moments people born in 2035 will grow up hearing about.

February 8

Chinese New Year Welcomes the Wood Rabbit

The Year of the Wood Rabbit gets underway, with celebrations, red envelopes, and 15 days of festivities worldwide. The Lantern Festival lands on Feb 22, 2035.

March 9

Annular Solar Eclipse

An annular eclipse crosses the Southern Ocean and parts of Antarctica. Skywatchers in the right spots see a "ring of fire" effect as the moon covers most but not all of the sun.

September 2

Total Solar Eclipse

The headline sky event of the year. The path of totality crosses China, the Koreas, and Japan, putting it in front of hundreds of millions of people. Astronomers expect a major tourism spike across East Asia.

October 14

Annular Solar Eclipse

A second solar eclipse of the year —this one annular —crosses the Pacific, Hawaii, and parts of Central America. A rare double-feature year for skywatchers.

December 7

Pearl Harbor Remembrance Day

The 84th anniversary of the attack on Pearl Harbor. Veterans' groups in Hawaii host a memorial service, as they do every year on the date that pulled the US into World War II.
