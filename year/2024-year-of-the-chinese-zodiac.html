2024 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 2024 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Jia Chen

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2024 was the year of Jia Chen. "Jia" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chen" maps to the Dragon in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 2024

The 2024 lunar year kicked off on Feb 10, 2024 and wrapped up on Jan 28, 2025. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Dragon. But if you were born earlier in Feb 2024 -- before the 10 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

It pushes things forward and gives people born under its influence a natural confidence and drive. Wood adds growth and cooperation. 2024 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 2024 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

2024 on the Western Calendar

2024 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Wood Dragon's growth-oriented, progressive energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 2024 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 2024, the Wood Dragon period ran from Feb 10, 2024 through Jan 28, 2025.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 10 2024 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Dragon. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters
