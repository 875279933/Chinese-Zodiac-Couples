1987 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1987 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Ding Mao

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1987 was the year of Ding Mao. "Ding" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Mao" maps to the Rabbit in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1987

The 1987 lunar year kicked off on Jan 29, 1987 and wrapped up on Feb 16, 1988. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Rabbit. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1987 -- before the 29 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1987 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1987 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1987 on the Western Calendar

1987 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Fire Rabbit's passionate, dynamic energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1987 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1987, the Fire Rabbit period ran from Jan 29, 1987 through Feb 16, 1988.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 29 1987 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Rabbit. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
