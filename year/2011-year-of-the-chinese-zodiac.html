2011 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2011 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Xin Mao

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2011 was the year of Xin Mao. "Xin" represents Yin Metal in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Mao" maps to the Rabbit in the Earthly Branches. The Xin Mao pairing shows up once every 60 years and gives the 2011 year of the Chinese zodiac a particular softness married to inner resolve.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2011 lunar year opened on Feb 3, 2011 and closed on Jan 22, 2012. Anyone born in that window carries the Metal Rabbit sign. Born earlier in February 2011 -- before the 3rd -- and you'd be the previous sign.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar follows the moon's phases, with each new month starting on a new moon. A standard year covers about 354 days, and an extra leap month is added every few years to keep pace with the sun.

Yin Metal Energy

Xin Metal is Yin Metal -- refined, contained, and quietly strong. Where Yang Metal is loud and outward, Yin Metal is patient and precise. It gives the 2011 year of the Chinese zodiac a polish and a sharpness that doesn't need to announce itself.

A Navy SEAL team raided bin Laden's compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan. The operation lasted 40 minutes and was watched live in the White House Situation Room. Obama announced it at 11:35 PM ET.

Prince William married Kate Middleton at Westminster Abbey, watched by an estimated 2 billion people globally. Kate's Alexander McQueen dress became the most-photographed wedding gown of the decade.
