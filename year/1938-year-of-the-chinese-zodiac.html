1938 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1938 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Wu Yin

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1938 was the year of Wu Yin. "Wu" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Yin" maps to the Tiger in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1938

The 1938 lunar year kicked off on Jan 31, 1938 and wrapped up on Feb 18, 1939. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Tiger. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1938 -- before the 31 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1938 in the Gregorian Calendar

1938 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Tiger's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1938 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1938, the Earth Tiger period ran from Jan 31, 1938 through Feb 18, 1939.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 31 1938 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Tiger. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
