2018 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 2018 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Wu Xu

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2018 was the year of Wu Xu. "Wu" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Xu" maps to the Dog in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 2018

The 2018 lunar year kicked off on Feb 16, 2018 and wrapped up on Feb 4, 2019. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Dog. But if you were born earlier in Feb 2018 -- before the 16 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

2018 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 2018 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

2018 on the Western Calendar

2018 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Dog's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 2018 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 2018, the Earth Dog period ran from Feb 16, 2018 through Feb 4, 2019.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 16 2018 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Dog. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters

If you're looking into family history or just trying to understand what the 2018 year of the Chinese zodiac really means, getting the calendar right is half the battle. The Chinese zodiac gives you a completely different way of reading personality and life path compared to Western astrology.
