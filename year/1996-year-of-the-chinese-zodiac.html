1996 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1996 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Bing Zi

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1996 was the year of Bing Zi. "Bing" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Zi" maps to the Rat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1996

The 1996 lunar year kicked off on Feb 19, 1996 and wrapped up on Feb 6, 1997. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Rat. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1996 -- before the 19 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1996 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1996 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1996 on the Western Calendar

1996 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Fire Rat's passionate, dynamic energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1996 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1996, the Fire Rat period ran from Feb 19, 1996 through Feb 6, 1997.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 19 1996 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Rat. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
