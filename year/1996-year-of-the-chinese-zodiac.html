1996 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1996 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Bing Zi

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1996 was the year of Bing Zi. "Bing" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Zi" maps to the Rat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 1996 lunar year kicked off on Feb 19, 1996 and wrapped up on Feb 6, 1997. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Rat. Born earlier in Feb 1996 -- before the 19 -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Yang Fire Energy

Bing Fire is Yang Fire -- active, outward, and assertive. It pushes things forward and gives people born under its influence a natural confidence and drive. Fire cranks up the intensity for 1996-born Rats.
