1940 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar do not follow the same rules. Here is how that affects the 1940 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Geng Chen

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1940 was the year of Geng Chen. "Geng" stands for Yang Metal in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chen" maps to the Dragon in the Earthly Branches. The pairing recurs once every 60 years, with the next Geng Chen year falling in 2000.

Lunar Year Dates for 1940

The 1940 lunar year kicked off on Feb 8, 1940 and wrapped up on Jan 26, 1941. A birthday inside that window is a Metal Dragon. A January 1940 birthday falls under the Earth Rabbit of the 1939 year of the Chinese zodiac, since the 1940 lunar year has not opened yet.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month is added roughly every three years to keep pace with the solar year.

Why Yang Metal Matters

The Geng Metal in the 1940 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yang Metal, like a sword or a strong axe. It shapes, it cuts, it lasts. Yang Metal gives the typical Dragon ambition a harder edge and a longer memory.

1940 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1940 look like through the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the 1940 year of the Chinese zodiac?

1940 on the Western Calendar

1940 on the Gregorian calendar runs January 1 through December 31. In Chinese culture, the year carried the Metal Dragon's focused, decisive energy, shaping how people born that year push a project across the line.

Why the Dates Do Not Line Up

The 1940 year of the Chinese zodiac does not run from January 1 to December 31. Chinese New Year moves around the Gregorian calendar, so the zodiac year spans two Gregorian years. For 1940, the Metal Dragon period ran from February 8, 1940 through January 26, 1941.

Check the Birthdate Carefully

Born February 7, 1940 or earlier? You are the Earth Rabbit, not a Dragon. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count, and it pays to double-check when the birthday falls close to the Chinese New Year cutoff.
