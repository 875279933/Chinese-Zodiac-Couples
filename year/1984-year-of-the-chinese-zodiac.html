1984 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1984 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Jia Zi

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1984 was the year of Jia Zi. "Jia" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Zi" maps to the Rat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1984

The 1984 lunar year kicked off on Feb 2, 1984 and wrapped up on Feb 19, 1985. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Rat. If you were born earlier in Feb 1984 -- before the 2 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why Yang Wood Matters

The Jia Wood in the 1984 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yang Wood -- Yang energy is active, outward, and assertive. It pushes things forward and gives people born under its influence a natural confidence and drive. Wood adds growth and cooperation to the Rat's sharp instincts.
