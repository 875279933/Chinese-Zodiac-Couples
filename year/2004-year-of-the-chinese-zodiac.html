2004 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2004 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Jia Shen

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2004 was the year of Jia Shen. "Jia" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Shen" maps to the Monkey in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2004 lunar year kicked off on Jan 22, 2004 and wrapped up on Feb 8, 2005. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Monkey. Born earlier in Jan 2004 -- before the 22 -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years.

Yang Wood Energy

Jia Wood is Yang Wood -- the tall tree, the trunk, the upward reach. It gives people born under its influence a drive to grow, expand, and lead from a solid base.
