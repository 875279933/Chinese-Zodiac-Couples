1986 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1986 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Bing Yin

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1986 was the year of Bing Yin. "Bing" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Yin" maps to the Tiger in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1986

The 1986 lunar year kicked off on Feb 9, 1986 and wrapped up on Jan 28, 1987. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Tiger. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1986 -- before the 9 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1986 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1986 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1986 on the Western Calendar

1986 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Fire Tiger's passionate, dynamic energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1986 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1986, the Fire Tiger period ran from Feb 9, 1986 through Jan 28, 1987.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 9 1986 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Tiger. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
