The Year of Ji Wei

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1979 was the year of Ji Wei. "Ji" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Wei" maps to the Goat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1979

The 1979 lunar year kicked off on Jan 28, 1979 and wrapped up on Feb 15, 1980. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Goat. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1979 -- before the 28 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1979 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1979 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1979 on the Western Calendar

1979 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Goat's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1979 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1979, the Earth Goat period ran from Jan 28, 1979 through Feb 15, 1980.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 28 1979 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Goat. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
