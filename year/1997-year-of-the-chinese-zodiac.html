1997 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1997 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Ding Chou

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1997 was the year of Ding Chou. "Ding" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chou" maps to the Ox in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 1997 lunar year kicked off on Feb 7, 1997 and wrapped up on Jan 27, 1998. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Ox. Born earlier in Feb 1997 -- before the 7 -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Yin Fire Energy

Ding Fire is Yin Fire -- receptive, inward, and intuitive. It gives people born under its influence a depth of feeling and a quiet strength that runs deeper than it appears.
