1972 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1972 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1972 was the year of Ren Zi. "Ren" stands for Water in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Zi" maps to the Rat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years -- the last time was 1912, and the next won't come until 2032.

Lunar Year Dates for 1972

The 1972 lunar year kicked off on Feb 15, 1972 and wrapped up on Feb 2, 1973. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Water Rat. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1972 -- before the 15 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1972 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1972, the Water Rat period ran from Feb 15, 1972 through Feb 2, 1973.
