1946 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1946 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Bing Xu

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1946 was the year of Bing Xu. "Bing" stands for Fire in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Xu" maps to the Dog in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1946

The 1946 lunar year kicked off on Feb 2, 1946 and wrapped up on Jan 21, 1947. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Fire Dog. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1946 -- before the 2 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1946 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1946 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1946 on the Western Calendar

1946 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Fire Dog's passionate, dynamic energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1946 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1946, the Fire Dog period ran from Feb 2, 1946 through Jan 21, 1947.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 2 1946 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Dog. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
