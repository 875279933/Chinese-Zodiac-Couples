2039 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2039 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Ji Wei

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2039 is the year of Ji Wei. "Ji" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems, and "Wei" maps to the Goat in the Earthly Branches. This combo only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2039 lunar year kicks off on Jan 23, 2039 and wraps up on Feb 11, 2040. If your birthday lands in that window, you're an Earth Goat. Born earlier in Jan 2039 -- before the 23rd -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

An annular eclipse crosses the Indian Ocean, Indonesia, and the western Pacific. Skywatchers in the path see the moon take a "bite" out of the sun, leaving a glowing ring overhead.
