1941 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1941 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Xin Si

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1941 was the year of Xin Si. "Xin" stands for Metal in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Si" maps to the Snake in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1941

The 1941 lunar year kicked off on Jan 27, 1941 and wrapped up on Feb 14, 1942. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Metal Snake. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1941 -- before the 27 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1941 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1941 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1941 on the Western Calendar

1941 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Metal Snake's determined, resolute energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1941 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1941, the Metal Snake period ran from Jan 27, 1941 through Feb 14, 1942.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 27 1941 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Snake. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
