1955 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1955 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Yi Wei

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1955 was the year of Yi Wei. "Yi" stands for Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Wei" maps to the Goat in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1955

The 1955 lunar year kicked off on Jan 24, 1955 and wrapped up on Feb 11, 1956. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Goat. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1955 -- before the 24 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Why Yin Wood Matters

The Yi Wood in the 1955 year of the Chinese zodiac is Yin Wood -- Yin energy is receptive, inward, and intuitive. It gives people born under its influence a depth of feeling and a quiet strength that runs deeper than it appears. Wood adds growth and cooperation. People born in this year tend to be more collaborative and open-minded than others of their sign, always looking for ways to expand and improve.

1955 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1955 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1955 on the Western Calendar

1955 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Wood Goat's growth-oriented, progressive energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1955 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1955, the Wood Goat period ran from Jan 24, 1955 through Feb 11, 1956.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 24 1955 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Goat. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters

If you're looking into family history or just trying to understand what the 1955 year of the Chinese zodiac really means, getting the calendar right is half the battle. The Chinese zodiac gives you a completely different way of reading personality and life path compared to the Western zodiac tradition.

Famous People Born in 1955

1955 had some quiet but massive stars. Here are six Earth Goats worth remembering.

Steve Jobs

Born February 24 in San Francisco. Jobs co-founded Apple in 1976 with Steve Wozniak, was forced out in 1985, came back in 1997, and launched the iPod (2001), iPhone (2007), and iPad (2010). Apple became the first company in history to hit $1 trillion in 2018.

Bill Gates

Born October 28 in Seattle. Gates co-founded Microsoft in 1975 at age 19, led the company to dominate personal computing in the 1990s, and stepped down as CEO in 2000. He's been the richest person in the world for over 20 of the last 30 years and has given away over $60 billion.

Bruce Willis

Born March 19 in Idar-Oberstein, West Germany. Willis played David Addison on Moonlighting (1985-1989), then John McClane in Die Hard (1988) and five sequels. The Sixth Sense (1999) earned him a third Golden Globe nomination and made him one of the highest-paid actors of the 2000s.

Kathy Bates

Born June 28 in Memphis. Bates won the Best Actress Oscar for Misery (1990), a Stephen King adaptation. She won a second Oscar for the short film Talk to Her in 2002 and an Emmy for American Horror Story in 2014.

Bill Paxton

Born May 17 in Fort Worth. Paxton had small roles in The Terminator and Aliens before bigger parts in Twister (1996), Apollo 13 (1995), and Titanic (1997). He headlined the HBO series Big Love from 2006 to 2011 and the History series Hatfields & McCoys.

Elton John

Born March 25 in Pinner. John co-wrote the music for The Lion King (1994) and won the Oscar for "Can You Feel the Love Tonight." His farewell tour in 2023 was the highest-grossing concert tour in history, pulling in over $900 million.
