2029 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 2029 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Ji You

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2029 was the year of Ji You. "Ji" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "You" maps to the Rooster in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 2029

The 2029 lunar year kicked off on Feb 13, 2029 and wrapped up on Feb 2, 2030. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Rooster. But if you were born earlier in Feb 2029 -- before the 13 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

2029 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 2029 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

2029 on the Western Calendar

2029 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Rooster's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 2029 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 2029, the Earth Rooster period ran from Feb 13, 2029 through Feb 2, 2030.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 13 2029 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Rooster. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Why This Distinction Matters
