1944 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1944 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Jia Shen

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1944 was the year of Jia Shen. "Jia" stands for Yang Wood in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Shen" maps to the Monkey in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1944

The 1944 lunar year kicked off on Jan 25, 1944 and wrapped up on Feb 12, 1945. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Wood Monkey. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1944 -- before the 25 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.
