1969 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1969 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Ji You

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1969 was the year of Ji You. "Ji" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "You" maps to the Rooster in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1969

The 1969 lunar year kicked off on Feb 17, 1969 and wrapped up on Feb 5, 1970. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Rooster. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1969 -- before the 17 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

People born in this year are more grounded and reliable than others of their sign, with a no-nonsense approach that gets results.

1969 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1969 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1969 on the Western Calendar

1969 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Rooster's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1969 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1969, the Earth Rooster period ran from Feb 17, 1969 through Feb 5, 1970.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 17 1969 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Rooster. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
