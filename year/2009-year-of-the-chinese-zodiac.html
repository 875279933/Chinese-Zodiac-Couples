2009 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2009 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Ji Chou

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2009 was the year of Ji Chou. "Ji" stands for Yin Earth in the Heavenly Stems, while "Chou" maps to the Ox in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only comes around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2009 lunar year kicked off on Jan 26, 2009 and wrapped up on Feb 13, 2010. If your birthday falls in that window, you're an Earth Ox. Born earlier in Jan 2009 -- before the 26 -- and you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets added roughly every three years, which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

Yin Earth Energy

Ji Earth is Yin Earth -- receptive, inward, and quietly steady. It gives people born under it a depth of feeling and a settled strength that runs deeper than it looks. Yin Earth grounds the Ox's natural dependability into something that doesn't shift.

Big Events That Shaped 2009

2009 was the year America got a new president and said goodbye to one of its biggest music stars. Here are five events that defined the year.

Jan 20, 2009

Obama Inaugurated

1.8 million people crowded the National Mall to watch Barack Obama take the oath of office as the 44th US president. Aretha Franklin sang and Biden was sworn in as VP.

June 25, 2009

Michael Jackson Dies

The King of Pop died at age 50 of cardiac arrest at his Holmby Hills home. His memorial at the Staples Center drew an estimated 2.5 billion viewers worldwide.

Jan 3, 2009

Bitcoin Network Goes Live

Satoshi Nakamoto mined the genesis block, kicking off the first decentralized cryptocurrency. The first real-world BTC transaction, 10,000 coins for two pizzas, came in May.

April 23, 2009

H1N1 Swine Flu Pandemic

The CDC reported the first US case in California. The WHO declared a global pandemic in June, with CDC estimates later putting US cases at 60.8 million and deaths at 12,469.

June 1, 2009

Air France Flight 447 Crashes

An Airbus A330 from Rio to Paris went down in the Atlantic with 228 people on board. The black box was recovered from 12,800 feet of water in 2011.
