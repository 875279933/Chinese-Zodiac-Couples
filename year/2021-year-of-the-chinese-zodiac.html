2021 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 2021 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Xin Chou

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2021 was the year of Xin Chou. "Xin" stands for Metal in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chou" maps to the Ox in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 2021

The 2021 lunar year kicked off on Feb 12, 2021 and wrapped up on Jan 31, 2022. If your birthday falls in that window, you're an Metal Ox. But if you were born earlier in Feb 2021 -- before the 12 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

2021 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 2021 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

2021 on the Western Calendar

2021 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Metal Ox's determined, resolute energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 2021 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 2021, the Metal Ox period ran from Feb 12, 2021 through Jan 31, 2022.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 12 2021 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not an Ox. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.

Big Events That Shaped 2021

2021 was the year America tried to turn the page. A mob stormed the Capitol, vaccines went into arms, and the US pulled out of its longest war. Here are five events that made the year.

Jan 6, 2021

Capitol Building Attacked

A mob of Trump supporters broke into the US Capitol while Congress was certifying the election. Five people died, 140 officers were injured, and it was the worst attack on the Capitol since the War of 1812. Four officers later died by suicide.

Jan 20, 2021

Biden Inaugurated 46th President

Biden was sworn in at a US Capitol ringed by 25,000 National Guard troops. Just 12 days earlier the building had been under attack. 40 million fewer viewers watched than in 2017.

Dec 14, 2020 - 2021

COVID Vaccines Roll Out

Pfizer shipped the first US-authorized vaccine on Dec 14, 2020. By April 19, 2021, 50% of US adults had at least one dose. The CDC approved vaccines for kids 5-11 on Nov 2, 2021.

July 23, 2021

Tokyo Olympics Held Without Fans

The first Summer Games postponed in Olympic history. Held a year late with no spectators at most venues. The US won 39 gold medals, second to China's 38, and the Tokyo organizing committee lost an estimated $15 billion.

Aug 15, 2021

Kabul Falls to Taliban

The Afghan government collapsed as the Taliban took Kabul just 11 days before the US planned to complete its withdrawal. 123,000 people were evacuated in 17 days -- the largest US airlift since the 1975 Vietnam fall of Saigon.
