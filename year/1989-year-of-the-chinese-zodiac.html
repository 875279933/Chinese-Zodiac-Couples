1989 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1989 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Ji Si

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1989 was the year of Ji Si. "Ji" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Si" maps to the Snake in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 1989 lunar year kicked off on Feb 6, 1989 and wrapped up on Jan 26, 1990. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Snake. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1989 -- before the 6 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. Standard year runs about 354 days; a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years.

Yin Earth Energy

Ji Earth is Yin Earth -- receptive, inward, and intuitive. It gives people born under its influence a depth of feeling and a quiet strength that runs deeper than it appears.
