1998 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar don't follow the same rules. Here's how that affects the 1998 year of the Chinese zodiac.

The Year of Wu Yin

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1998 was the year of Wu Yin. "Wu" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Yin" maps to the Tiger in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates for 1998

The 1998 lunar year kicked off on Jan 28, 1998 and wrapped up on Feb 15, 1999. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Earth Tiger. But if you were born earlier in Jan 1998 -- before the 28 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1998 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1998 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1998 on the Western Calendar

1998 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Tiger's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1998 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1998, the Earth Tiger period ran from Jan 28, 1998 through Feb 15, 1999.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Jan 28 1998 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Tiger. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
