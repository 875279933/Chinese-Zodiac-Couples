1978 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1978 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1978 was the year of Wu Wu. "Wu" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Wu" maps to the Horse in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Calendar System

The 1978 lunar year kicked off on Feb 7, 1978 and wrapped up on Jan 27, 1979. If your birthday falls in that window, you're an Earth Horse. But if you were born earlier in Feb 1978 -- before the 7 -- you'd actually be the previous zodiac sign instead.

How the Lunar Calendar Works

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases, with each month starting on the new moon. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. To keep pace with the solar year, a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years -- which is why Chinese New Year jumps around on the Western calendar.

1978 on the Western Calendar

1978 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. But in Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Horse's grounded, stable energy, shaping how people born that year see the world.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1978 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. Because Chinese New Year shifts each year, the zodiac year straddles two Gregorian years. For 1978, the Earth Horse period ran from Feb 7, 1978 through Jan 27, 1979.

Check Your Birthdate Carefully

This matters more than most people realize. Born Feb 7 1978 or earlier? You're the previous sign, not a Horse. The lunar calendar dates are the ones that count -- always double-check if your birthday falls near the Chinese New Year cutoff.
