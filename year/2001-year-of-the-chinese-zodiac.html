2001 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 2001 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Xin Si

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 2001 was the year of Xin Si. "Xin" stands for Metal in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Si" maps to the Snake in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 2001 lunar year kicked off on Jan 24, 2001 and wrapped up on Feb 11, 2002. If your birthday falls in that window, you're a Metal Snake. Born earlier in Jan 2001 -- before the 24 -- you'd be the previous zodiac sign instead.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. A standard lunar year runs about 354 days across 12 months. A leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years.

Yin Metal Energy

Xin Metal is Yin Metal -- refined, polished, and precise. It gives people born under its influence a sharp mind and an eye for detail.
