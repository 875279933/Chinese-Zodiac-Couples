1988 Lunar Calendar Explained

The Chinese lunar calendar and the Gregorian calendar run on different tracks. That gap is why the 1988 year of the Chinese zodiac starts and ends on different dates than the Western calendar suggests.

The Year of Wu Chen

In the traditional Chinese lunar calendar, 1988 was the year of Wu Chen. "Wu" stands for Earth in the Heavenly Stems system, while "Chen" maps to the Dragon in the Earthly Branches. This particular pairing only rolls around once every 60 years.

Lunar Year Dates

The 1988 lunar year kicked off on Feb 17, 1988 and wrapped up on Feb 5, 1989. If your birthday falls in that window, you're an Earth Dragon.

Lunar System

The Chinese lunar calendar tracks the moon's phases. Standard year runs about 354 days; a leap month gets tacked on roughly every three years.

Yang Earth Energy

Wu Earth is Yang Earth -- active, outward, and assertive. It gives people born under its influence a natural confidence and grounded drive.

1988 in the Gregorian Calendar

What does 1988 look like through the lens of the Western calendar, and why does the date cutoff matter for the Chinese zodiac?

1988 on the Western Calendar

1988 on the Gregorian calendar is straightforward -- January 1 through December 31. In Chinese culture, the year pulsed with the Earth Dragon's grounded, stable energy.

Why the Dates Don't Line Up

The 1988 year of the Chinese zodiac doesn't run from January 1 to December 31. The Earth Dragon period ran from Feb 17, 1988 through Feb 5, 1989.

Check Your Birthdate
