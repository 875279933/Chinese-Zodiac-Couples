Personality Traits

Roosters are the ones who show up put-together, on time, and three steps ahead. They take pride in their work, their look, and their reputation. Loud, sure - but usually right.

Sharp Confidence

Roosters walk into a room like they own it - because they usually do. They know what they're good at, they don't pretend otherwise, and that kind of certainty is magnetic. Some call it cocky; the Rooster calls it honest.

Tidy Mind

Roosters like things in order. Their desk, their schedule, their to-do list - everything has a place. They're the ones who show you where the label maker is and aren't joking. Loose ends bother them more than most people.

Workhorse

Roosters outwork the room. They show up early, stay late, and they don't really get the "it's good enough" attitude. They want it done right, and they want to be the one who did it. Praise is nice, but the work itself is the reward.

Eagle Eye

Roosters catch the typo, the off color, the inconsistency nobody else noticed. It's a curse as much as a gift - they're hard to fool, and they have a hard time letting sloppy work slide. They make great editors, terrible liars.

Blunt

Roosters tell you what they think, whether you want to hear it or not. They're not being mean - they just don't see the point in sugarcoating. People who love them love the directness; people who don't tend to be people who wanted a lie.

Show-Off

Roosters like to be seen. Not in a desperate way - they just put in the effort and they want it noticed. The outfit, the dinner, the presentation - they care about how things land. Most of the time, the care pays off.

Roosters are the people you want on your team and slightly exhausting at a dinner party. They bring the standard, the deadline, the follow-through - and they expect the same from the people around them. The flip side is they can be too hard on themselves, and on others. The healthiest Rooster learns that not every room needs their polish.

Compatibility & Matches

Roosters need a partner who can handle the honesty and match the energy. The wrong match gets their feathers ruffled; the right one thrives on the directness.
